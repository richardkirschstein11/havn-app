## Key question 1: What material specification (classes, labels, test reports) should a European buyer demand from a Chinese cabin manufacturer to credibly market a "healthy, low-emission" cabin?

### Takeaway
From 6 August 2026 the EU formaldehyde restriction (REACH Annex XVII entry 77, Regulation (EU) 2023/1464) is the binding legal floor for any furniture/wood-based article placed on the EU market: ≤0.062 mg/m³ by chamber test, i.e. roughly half the old E1 limit and close to China's E0 grade (≤0.050 mg/m³). The credible "healthy" specification is therefore: ENF-grade (≤0.025 mg/m³) or at minimum E0 panels with chamber test reports, plus a German AgBB-2024 VOC emission report (the scheme that German building law via MVV TB Annex 8 treats as proof of health protection in occupied rooms), plus REACH/BPR documentation for any coatings, foams and "antimicrobial" additives.

### Cited Findings
Formaldehyde – EU legal baseline
- REACH Annex XVII entry 77 (added by Regulation (EU) 2023/1464) bans placing articles on the market after 6 August 2026 if formaldehyde released under the Appendix 14 chamber test exceeds 0.062 mg/m³ for furniture and wood-based articles, or 0.080 mg/m³ for other articles; road-vehicle interiors must meet ≤0.062 mg/m³ from 6 August 2027 (ISO 12219-1/-10 ambient mode) — [CIRS](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/chemicals/the-use-of-formaldehyde-and-formaldehyde-releasing-substances-will-be-restricted-under-reach); [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/knowledge-hub/technical-updates/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/eu-reach-annex-xvii-restriction-on-formaldehyde-emission-was-published); [Bureau Veritas](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/reach-restriction-formaldehyde-and-formaldehyde-releasers)
- Exemptions: articles where formaldehyde is exclusively naturally present in the source material, articles exclusively for outdoor use; biocidal products, medical devices, food-contact materials and PPE are excluded because they fall under their own frameworks — [CATAS](https://catas.com/en-GB/news/exemptions-for-the-new-european-reach-restriction-on-formaldehyde); [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/formaldehyde-testing-and-regulations-in-the-eu/)
- The 0.062 mg/m³ limit corresponds to half of the E1 emission class in Appendix B of EN 13986 (the harmonised wood-based-panel standard) — [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/formaldehyde-testing-and-regulations-in-the-eu/)
- Compliance is assessed with the chamber method described in Appendix 14 of the restriction — [CIRS](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/chemicals/the-use-of-formaldehyde-and-formaldehyde-releasing-substances-will-be-restricted-under-reach)

Formaldehyde – Chinese grading the supplier will quote
- China's GB/T 39600-2021 (effective 1 October 2021) grades indoor wood-based panels and finished products into E1 (≤0.124 mg/m³), E0 (≤0.050 mg/m³) and ENF (≤0.025 mg/m³); scope covers fibreboard (MDF), particleboard, plywood, blockboard, LVL, glulam, wood flooring, wall panelling and wood doors — [Intertek China](https://www.intertek.com.cn/eninfo_itemid_206574_lcid_40.html)
- GB/T 39600 is a recommended (voluntary) standard; a 2026 Beijing Business Daily report says the new mandatory national standard GB 18580-2025 upgraded E0 from recommended to mandatory — [Beijing Daily 2026](https://news.bjd.com.cn/2026/05/21/11757987.shtml); a 2021 report had instead described GB/T 39600 and GB/T 39598 as mandatory — [Beijing Business Daily 2021](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2021/0917/412567.shtml) (sources conflict on legal status; the mandatory instrument is GB 18580)
- ENF is not "zero formaldehyde": it still permits release up to 0.025 mg/m³ — [NBD 2021](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2021-09-27/1926909.html)

VOC – the German AgBB scheme (what German authorities accept)
- The AgBB evaluation procedure for VOC emissions from building products, September 2024 edition, incorporates the 2022 LCI (NIK) value list; the previous version remains valid for one more year, and old and new versions may not be mixed — [UBA AgBB 2024 (EN)](https://umweltbundesamt.de/sites/default/files/medien/4031/dokumente/agbb_evaluation_scheme_2024.pdf)
- The scheme limits carcinogens at 3 and 28 days, TVOC at 3 and 28 days, TSVOC at 28 days, and individual VOCs via NIK (LCI) values / R-value at 28 days — [Eurofins/DIBt summary](https://www.eurofins.com/media/1562/agbb-dibt-approvals-de.pdf)
- MVV TB Annex 8 "Anforderungen an bauliche Anlagen hinsichtlich des Gesundheitsschutzes (ABG)" has required VOC-emission proof for numerous building products used in Aufenthaltsräume (habitable/occupied rooms) since 2017 and is adopted in many Landesbauordnungen; the basis is the AgBB scheme — [Baunetz Wissen](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/holz/fachwissen/holz-und-raumluft/richt--und-leitwerte-fuer-voc-8637596); [UBA](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/themen/neues-agbb-schema-zur-bewertung-von-bauprodukten)
- Per the 2026 Bundesgesundheitsblatt publication of the scheme, where the AgBB criteria are met the minimum requirements of the state building codes for protecting user health against VOC emissions in occupied rooms are deemed fulfilled — [UBA Bundesgesundheitsblatt 2026](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/system/files/document/AgBB-Bewertungsschema%202026_Bundesgesundheitsblatt.pdf)

Antimicrobial additives in materials (silver-ion etc.) – legal preconditions
- Under the Biocidal Products Regulation (EU) 528/2012 Art. 58, a treated article may be placed on the EU market only if all active substances in the biocidal products it was treated with are on the approved-substance list and any conditions of approval are met; labelling is mandatory where the manufacturer claims biocidal properties (statement that it incorporates biocidal products, the substantiated property, the active-substance names) — [BPR Chapter XIII text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/528/chapter/XIII); [KEMI fact sheet](https://www.kemi.se/download/18.164ad6b3172927a92893292a/1594972056851/facts-articles-treated-with-biocides2.pdf)
- Commission guidance: whether a biocide is "treated with" or "intentionally incorporated" makes little practical difference under Art. 58 — only approved active substances may be used and labelling applies when a claim is made — [Commission CA-Sept13-Doc 5.1.e treated articles guidance](https://ust.is/library/Skrar/Atvinnulif/Efni/Saefiefni/CA-Sept13-Doc%205.1.e%20(Rev1)%20-%20treated%20articles%20guidance.pdf)
- Silver zinc zeolite was approved as an active substance for product-types 2, 7 and 9 subject to conditions, including restrictions on treating textiles that may contact skin, be handled wet or be mouthed by young children — [Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2635](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2024/2635/oj)

Benchmark: what a leading European pod maker specifies
- Framery (Finland) states its interior sandwich panels contain recycled acoustic foam and acoustic felt — [Framery One](https://www.frameryacoustics.com/en/products/framery-one); resellers list the acoustic foam as B-s1,d0 (EN 13501-1), the acoustic felt as EN 13501-1 classified and the floor carpet as Cfl-s1 — [HuntOffice Framery O](https://www.huntoffice.ie/Framery-O-Standard-Phone-Booth.html)

### Inferences
- Demand checklist (derived from the findings above):
  1. Panel formaldehyde: ENF per GB/T 39600-2021 (≤0.025 mg/m³) or at least E0 (≤0.050), AND a chamber test report per the REACH Appendix 14 method (EN 16516/EN 717-1-type 1 m³ chamber) showing ≤0.062 mg/m³ for the finished furniture article, dated and from an ISO 17025 lab (SGS/Intertek/TÜV/CATAS). Reason: after 6 Aug 2026 the REACH limit is a condition of lawful sale, not a marketing extra.
  2. VOC: a full AgBB-2024 (28-day) emission test report on the assembled wall/ceiling/floor build-ups (not just raw board), because MVV TB Annex 8 and the state building codes treat AgBB compliance as the health-protection benchmark for occupied rooms.
  3. Foams/textiles/adhesives: REACH SVHC declaration (<0.1 % w/w), and a statement that no restricted phthalates (Annex XVII entry 51/52) or flame-retardant SVHCs are used (UNVERIFIED detail, see Gaps).
  4. Any "antimicrobial" surface: name the active substance, its BPR approval status for the relevant product-type (PT 2 surface disinfection / PT 9 fibres, polymers), and Art. 58-compliant labelling text; if the supplier cannot name an approved substance, strike the claim.
  5. Fire classes per EN 13501-1 for every exposed lining (see KQ3) — same test campaign can be combined with the emission tests.
  6. Natural/biophilic upgrade path (interpretation): replace melamine-faced MDF with E0/ENF plywood or solid-wood veneer on low-emission core, PET-felt acoustic panels with declared recycled content and AgBB report, linoleum or cork flooring with Cfl-s1 class, wool-felt where fire class is proven; the Framery build-up (foam + felt sandwich, Cfl-s1 carpet) is the market reference the Chinese supplier should match or exceed.
- The conflicting Chinese reports on "mandatory vs. recommended" should be handled by asking the supplier to state the grade per both GB/T 39600-2021 and GB 18580 (current edition) and to supply the test report rather than relying on the label.

## Key question 2: Which antimicrobial and air-cleaning technologies have evidence and are safe for unattended use, and which are discouraged (ozone, ionisers, UV-C exposure)?

### Takeaway
The evidence hierarchy for a self-service pod is: mechanical fresh-air purge between guests and clean washable textiles (robust, no safety downside) > UV-C in enclosed, occupancy-interlocked form (good germicidal evidence, but the device must comply with LVD/IEC 60335-2-65-type safety and the Directive 2006/25/EC exposure limit of 30 J/m² effective per day if staff could be exposed) > far-UVC 222 nm (safety data supportive within 2022 ACGIH limits, but ozone/VOC by-product and long-term questions remain open) > copper/silver surfaces (lab efficacy consistent, infection-reduction evidence weak and BPR labelling applies) >> ozone generators and ionisers (German IRK/UBA and consumer bodies discourage them; peer-reviewed data show negligible benefit and by-products).

### Cited Findings
Copper and other antimicrobial surfaces
- A 2023 systematic review (Aillón-García et al., American Journal of Infection Control; 15 studies 2010–2022, Newcastle-Ottawa quality assessment) found copper-treated surfaces were not clearly shown to reduce infections; roughly two-thirds of included studies reported a significant drop in microbial burden on copper objects vs. controls, and the two highest-quality studies suggested copper can lower bacterial contamination on touch surfaces; conclusions were hedged as "potential effectiveness" — [Universidad de Navarra record](https://academica-e.unavarra.es/xmlui/handle/2454/45997); [Clínica Universidad de Navarra summary](https://www.cun.es/en/research/scientific-publications/effectiveness-copper-preventive-tool-healthcare-facilities)
- Muller et al. 2016 (J Hosp Infect) systematic review of antimicrobial surfaces: copper produced only modest reductions in contamination in clinical settings; the single ICU RCT reporting a 58 % reduction in HCAI (p = 0.013) was rated low quality because of randomisation and blinding problems — [Muller 2016 abstract](https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/239675/antimicrobial-surfaces-to-prevent-healthcare-associated-infections-a-systematic-review); earlier review of copper efficacy in healthcare — [O'Gorman/Humphreys-type review, PMC4474853](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4474853/)
- A medRxiv systematic review of controlled trials judged all included copper-surface studies to be at high risk of bias in ≥2 of 7 domains — [medRxiv](https://medrxiv.org/node/82949)
- Legal: silver/copper antimicrobial coatings are "treated articles" under BPR Art. 58 — only approved active substances, labelling when a claim is made (see KQ1 citations) — [BPR Chapter XIII](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2012/528/chapter/XIII)

Far-UVC (222 nm)
- Javorsky et al., "Assessing the safety of new germicidal far-UVC technologies", Photochemistry and Photobiology (2024): far-UVC = 200–235 nm; current evidence supports use within existing guidelines, but the upper safety limit still needs study; authors call for mechanism-of-harm work, action spectra, and high-dose long-term exposure trials; ACGIH revised its 8-h exposure limits in 2022 with separate eye and skin limits — [PubMed 37929787](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37929787/); [FBH Berlin record](https://www.fbh-berlin.de/forschung/publikationen/assessing-the-safety-of-new-germicidal-far-uvc-technologies)
- Ozone: a 2023 Science of the Total Environment study (University of Colorado) concluded filtered KrCl* excimer far-UVC devices "should be safe to be applied in occupied spaces, with limited ozone generation" — [Ma et al. 2023 PDF](https://www.colorado.edu/ceae/sites/default/files/attached-files/ma_222_reflection_stoten_2023.pdf)
- A hotel-room study (Baltimore) found 222 nm far-UVC increased indoor ozone, though levels remained below outdoor ozone — reported in search summary; probable source [CDC Stacks document](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/257928/cdc_257928_DS1.pdf) (not opened; attribution unconfirmed)
- A secondary article (2026) reports NIST measurements showing 34 VOCs rising during far-UVC operation and stresses that an unfiltered KrCl lamp emits a hazardous longer-wavelength tail — [Disabled World](https://www.disabled-world.com/disability/publications/journals/222-nm.php) (secondary source of uneven quality; verify against NIST primary)
- FDA safety summary cites long-term hairless-mouse studies (incl. 66 weeks of exposure) and a human study with no long-term adverse ocular events — [FDA document](https://www.fda.gov/media/190275/download)

Conventional UV-C (254 nm) – EU safety framework
- Directive 2006/25/EC (artificial optical radiation) sets worker exposure limit values for eyes and skin; employers must assess and if necessary measure/calculate exposure levels (Art. 4) — [Directive 2006/25/EC Art. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2006/25/article/4); the maximum daily effective dose is Heff = 30 J/m² weighted over 180–400 nm per Annex I Table 1.2 — [sglux UV-C datasheet citing the Directive](https://www.boselec.com/wp-content/uploads/Linear/sglux/sgluxLiterature/Safester-UVC-BEC-1.pdf)
- IEC PAS 63313:2021 (position statement on germicidal UV-C irradiation, UV-C safety guidelines) applies to UV-C sources/products emitting 180–280 nm and defers to product standards IEC 60335-2-65 (UV-C air disinfection appliances) and IEC 60335-2-109 (UV-C water disinfection) — [IEC webstore](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/68489); [Czech standards summary](https://technickenormy.cz/iec-pas-63313-2021-position-statement-on-germicidal-uv-c-irradiation-uv-c-safety-guidelines-2)
- Spanish specification UNE 0068:2020 for UV-C apparatus points to the Low Voltage Directive and Directive 2006/25/EC and requires the declaration of conformity to cover any other applicable harmonised legislation — [UNE 0068 (EN)](https://www.sanidad.gob.es/profesionales/saludPublica/ccayes/alertasActual/nCov/documentos/Especificacion_UNE_0068-2020(EN).pdf)
- Chemical disinfectants (not UV) are regulated as biocidal products under Regulation (EU) 528/2012; qualification is based on intended use — [ANSM](https://ansm.sante.fr/dossiers-thematiques/status-of-disinfectants-used-in-the-medical-sector/impression/47)

Ionisers / "plasma" / ozone
- Zeng et al. 2021 (Building and Environment 195:107750) tested an in-duct bipolar ioniser: almost no impact on indoor air quality; decreased some irritants (xylenes, ethylbenzene) but increased others (acetone) and created poorly understood fragments; ions can react with indoor compounds to form by-products such as formaldehyde and ozone; net health impact "could be positive, negative, or negligible" — [Illinois Tech](https://www.iit.edu/news/Joint-university-research-finds-some-air-purifiers-may-actually-increase-harmful-airborne-chemicals); [Colorado State](https://newsmediarelations.colostate.edu/2021/03/30/study-uncovers-safety-concerns-with-some-air-purifiers-marketed-for-covid-19/)
- California requires indoor air cleaners sold since 2010 to meet ozone emission limits (time-weighted average below 0.050 ppmv over 8 h in a 30 m³ chamber) — [Illinois Tech](https://www.iit.edu/news/Joint-university-research-finds-some-air-purifiers-may-actually-increase-harmful-airborne-chemicals)
- German Innenraumlufthygiene-Kommission (IRK) at UBA, 2015 statement (Bundesgesundheitsblatt): it cannot recommend indoor air cleaners that promise to remove or chemically convert gaseous pollutants, odours or bioaerosols until standardised test methods prove efficacy and emission-free operation under indoor conditions — [quoted in Auto Service Praxis](https://www.autoservicepraxis.de/downloadfile/1821421/90007_2016_20_07_M_044-045_n32_Geruchsbeseitigung_Teil_2_109914_print.pdf) (secondhand quotation)
- IRK statement of 16 Nov 2020 on mobile air purifiers: lists ozone, plasma and ionisation among methods in use; mobile purifiers cannot replace ventilation because they remove neither CO2 nor humidity — [UBA IRK 2020](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/system/files/medien/2546/dokumente/201116_irk_stellungnahme_luftreiniger.pdf)
- Verbraucherzentrale Hamburg advises against devices using ozone or ionisers, mainly because of chemical by-products affecting allergy sufferers; UBA treats ventilation as the most effective indoor-air measure — [Utopia summary](https://utopia.de/ratgeber/sind-luftreiniger-sinnvoll-das-solltest-du-wissen/)

Reset and linen practice at existing self-service sleep-cabin operators
- Napcabs at Frankfurt Airport: Napcabs GmbH is responsible for cleaning and disinfecting the cabins immediately after use and for maintenance — [Fraport 2020](https://www.fraport.com/en/newsroom/service-news/2020/napcabs-offer-peace-and-privacy.html)
- Napcabs at Berlin BER (2022): after each use the cleaning staff is automatically notified to tidy the cabin and make the bed with fresh linen; only then is the cabin released to the next passenger — [International Airport Review](https://www.internationalairportreview.com/news/173997/berlin-airport-launches-napcabs-for-passenger-relaxation)
- Napcabs' booking system initiates the cleaning process; no reception staff on site; cabins advertised as "always clean and tidy with fresh bed linen" (marketing claim) — [Napcabs](https://www.napcabs.com/for-guests)
- Airport Dimensions sleep cabins at Hamad International: "disinfected after every guest check-out" — [Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/business-travel/2020/09/17/airport-dimensions-debuts-lounge-with-sleep-cabins-at-hamad-international-airport/)
- German workplace rule ASR A4.2 (rest facilities for pregnant/nursing staff): couches must be upholstered and have a washable or disposable cover; privacy must be preserved — [KomNet 42495](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/42495)

### Inferences
- A 20-minute unattended reset cannot be "automatic linen change" in the literal sense at any operator found: Napcabs, the closest German precedent, uses a human cleaner triggered by the booking system; the realistic HAVN design is (i) a washable, zipped mattress encasement plus disposable or single-use-laundered fitted sheet/pillow cover that the cleaner swaps, (ii) a high-rate fresh-air purge during the reset window, (iii) optional enclosed UV-C with door/occupancy interlock, logged for each cycle.
- Far-UVC is not yet a "set and forget" technology for a 10 m³ enclosed pod: the open issues are ozone/VOC by-products in a small, poorly mixed volume and the unresolved upper exposure limit; if used, run only during the unoccupied reset, with a filtered KrCl lamp and ozone monitoring, and treat it as an extra, not the primary hygiene barrier.
- Ozone generators, "plasma" ionisers and photocatalytic devices without third-party emission test reports should be excluded from the spec: the German IRK position and the Zeng study give the operator no defensible basis, and by-products accumulate fastest in a small sealed pod.
- Copper or silver surfaces are a marketing risk in the EU: the infection-prevention evidence is weak, and any "antimicrobial" claim triggers BPR Art. 58 labelling; if used at all, limit to door handles/switches with a named approved active substance.
- Staff exposure: if any UV-C source can be on while a cleaner is inside, the operator becomes an employer under Directive 2006/25/EC (German OStrV, UNVERIFIED transposition name) and must do an exposure assessment; interlocks that make exposure impossible remove this burden.

### Gaps

- No source retrieved on mattress encasements and bed-bug prevention effectiveness (search not run). UNVERIFIED prior knowledge: certified bed-bug-proof encasements (e.g., tested to the "Bug-Lock"/Wisconsin protocol) are standard hotel practice; heat treatment >45 °C is the main remediation; UV-C does not control bed bugs.

## Key question 3: What fire class and safety features are realistically required for 11 pods inside an office or station building in Berlin, and which standards/test reports does the manufacturer need?

### Takeaway
German building law does not have a product category "sleeping pod": the pods are either treated as furniture/loose installations (then the building's own fire concept and the workplace rules govern) or, if the authority or fire-safety engineer treats them as room-in-room structures, they must prove their linings are at least "schwerentflammbar" (B-s1,d0/C-s1,d0 band under EN 13501-1; UNVERIFIED mapping, see Gaps) and fit into the sprinkler/fire-alarm layout. Realistic baseline for Berlin: EN 13501-1 reports for every lining (target B-s1,d0, floor Cfl-s1), a documented decision on sprinkler heads/smoke detection inside each pod agreed with the building's Brandschutzkonzept author and the insurer (VdS CEA 4001:2024), doors openable from inside without a key, and an electrical cut-off/fault reporting per the building's rules. Eleven sleeping pods with ≤12 beds stay below the Berlin "Beherbergungsstätte >12 Gastbetten" special-building threshold, but the host building is very likely already a Sonderbau (office rooms >400 m² or storey >1,600 m²).

### Cited Findings
Fire classification and what pod makers prove
- Framery states fire safety of its surface, upholstery and acoustic materials is tested and classified to national or international standards and that the need for end devices (detectors/sprinklers) inside the pod is always determined by the applicable local authorities — [Framery help: fire safety](https://help.framery.com/officepods/en/oxy_ex-1/common/safety/fire_safety_O.html)
- Reseller listing for Framery O: acoustic foam meets B-s1,d0 (EN 13501-1), acoustic felt EN 13501-1 classified, floor carpet Cfl-s1; Inspecta (EU-accredited inspection body) stated there is no need to install sprinklers in Framery O or Q — [HuntOffice](https://www.huntoffice.ie/Framery-O-Standard-Phone-Booth.html); [HuntOffice Q&A](https://help.huntoffice.ie:443/en/articles/2728418-framery-q-a-s) (reseller/marketing statements; underlying certificate not retrieved)
- US route for comparison: ICC-ES AC519 (acceptance criteria for prefabricated pods) requires ASTM E84/UL 723 interior finish, ASTM E648/NFPA 253 floor, Cal TB 117-2013 upholstery and full-scale fire testing — [ICC-ES AC519](https://icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/06-AC519-1020-R2-1.pdf)
- Musterbauordnung (model code; states adopt it into Landesbauordnungen): building materials are classed non-combustible, flame-retardant (schwerentflammbar) or normally combustible; highly flammable (leichtentflammbar) materials may not be used unless, combined with other materials, they are no longer highly flammable — [MBO excerpt](https://content.voltimum.com/de/137598/200704102282007041087008teil3.pdf) (2002 text; check current BauO Bln §26)

## Key question 4: Which CE directives apply to a pod with built-in lighting, ventilation and electronics, and who is the "manufacturer" under EU law when a German operator imports from China?

### Takeaway
A pod with mains-powered lighting, fans and electronics is an electrical product under the Low Voltage Directive 2014/35/EU and EMC Directive 2014/30/EU (plus RoHS 2011/65/EU and, for any Wi-Fi/Bluetooth module, RED 2014/53/EU); the construction shell itself is not a construction product under the CPR when freestanding. Under the EU "manufacturer" definition, the party that has the product designed or manufactured and markets it under its own name or trademark is the manufacturer with full conformity obligations — so a German operator branding the Guangzhou-built pod as its own product becomes the EU manufacturer, not merely an importer.

### Cited Findings
- EU product-safety definition of "manufacturer" (GPSR 2023/988, mirrored in the New Legislative Framework): "any natural or legal person who manufactures a product or has a product designed or manufactured, and markets that product under that person's name or trademark"; also any person who places a product on the market under their own name/trademark or substantially modifies it — [Westwood Products summary of GPSR](https://westwood-products.odoo.com/blog/producten-13/who-is-responsible-37); [Fruugo compliance wiki](https://fruugo.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/RR/pages/3809411104)
- OEM pattern described for China sourcing: one company commissions another to manufacture to its specifications and affix its trademark; the commissioning party sells under its own name — and under the Blue Guide framework is then treated as manufacturer — [ExamineChina](https://www.examinechina.com/what-is-oem/)
- The Blue Guide 2022 is the Commission's central (non-binding) reference on economic-operator obligations and conformity assessment; the EU legal acts remain authoritative — [WKO Austria](https://www.wko.at/kommunikationselektronik/leitfaden-fuer-die-umsetzung-der-produktvorschriften)
- Typical EU Declaration of Conformity structure for electrical/communication equipment: lists the Low Voltage Directive and EMC Directive as applicable legislation, issued under the manufacturer's sole responsibility, mapping to harmonised standards — safety EN 62368-1:2014+A11:2017, EMC EN 55032 / EN 55035, EN IEC 61000-3-2 / EN 61000-3-3; RoHS tabulated separately — [Unify DoC](https://wiki.unify.com/images/d/df/EU-DoC_25-101_OpenScape_Desk_Phone_CP_IP-Phones.pdf); [Zenitel DoC 2024 citing EN 62368-1:2023](https://www.zenitel.com/sites/default/files/2024-10/1022100100%20-%20CE.pdf)

  - Low Voltage Directive 2014/35/EU: whole electrical assembly (230 V distribution, drivers, fans, control PCB). Likely harmonised standards: EN 60335-1 + EN 60335-2-80 (fans) for appliance-type parts; EN 60598-1 (+ -2-x) for integrated luminaires; EN 62368-1 for the controller/tablet/AV electronics; IEC/EN 60335-2-65 if a UV-C air unit is built in; EN 62471 photobiological safety for LEDs and UV sources. ## Key question 5: Which claims can be made for sleep/health features without entering MDR scope, and are there precedents?

### Takeaway
MDR scope is driven by the manufacturer's stated intended purpose: a product becomes a medical device if its intended purpose is diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease (Art. 2(1)); Annex XVI then captures listed non-medical aesthetic products (e.g., lasers/IPL for skin treatment) regardless of claims. Precedent shows both routes: Withings deliberately took its Sleep Analyzer through CE medical certification to claim clinically validated sleep-apnoea detection, while red-light and sauna/wellness makers stay outside MDR by framing products as "general wellness and fitness" with no disease wording. For a commercial pod, "rest", "relaxation", "a calm, dark, quiet place to nap", "supports your routine" are the safe register; "treats insomnia", "improves recovery from injury", "extends lifespan/longevity" are disease- or physiology-altering claims that invite MDR classification and, separately, unfair-commercial-practice and (in Germany) Heilmittelwerbegesetz scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
- The European Commission states that MDR 2017/745 also covers certain devices without an intended medical purpose that are similar to medical devices in functioning and risk profile; the groups are listed in Annex XVI — [European Commission](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medical-devices-topics-interest/reprocessing-medical-devices/manufacturers-devices-without-intended-medical-purpose_en)
- Common specifications for Annex XVI groups are set by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2346, amended by (EU) 2023/1194 — [EUR-Lex 2022/2346](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2022/2346/oj/fra/pdf); [BSI Annex XVI white paper](https://bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/insights/white-papers/bsi-md-mdr-annex-xvi-whitepaper-en-gb.pdf)
- Annex XVI group 5 covers "equipment emitting high intensity electromagnetic radiation (e.g. infra-red, visible light and ultraviolet) intended for use on the human body, including coherent and non-coherent sources, monochromatic and broad spectrum, such as lasers and intense pulsed light equipment, for skin resurfacing, tattoo or hair removal or other skin treatment" — [BSI Annex XVI webinar](https://v1.bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/media/webinars/bsi-md-mdr-annex-xvi-devices-without-an-intended-medical-purpose.pdf) (group wording as summarised; LED red-light panels are not named expressly)
- Regulatory consultancy Obelis argues that red and blue light devices which make therapy claims must comply with MDR 2017/745 — tying the obligation to the claim rather than the hardware — [Obelis](https://www.obelis.net/news/red-and-blue-light-therapy-devices-and-eu-compliance/) (vendor view)
- Wellsystem (German wellness-equipment maker) states its non-medical products are "general wellness and fitness devices" whose intended use "does not fall within the scope of Article 2(1) of Regulation (EU) 2017/745 (MDR)" — [Wellsystem](https://wellsystem.com/de-en/shared-exhibitions/) (company self-assessment)
- Withings Sleep Analyzer launched in the EU and UK after receiving CE marking as a medical device, marketed as clinically validated for sleep-apnoea detection; a 118-patient study against polysomnography gave mean AHI 32.5 vs 32.8 — [Med-Tech News](https://www.med-technews.com/news/withings-launches-sleep-apnoea-tracker-and-analyser/); [Mobihealthnews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/europe/french-firm-withings-launches-under-mattress-sleep-tracking-mat)
- Withings' own FAQ still instructs users that if results show signs of sleep apnoea they should consult a doctor who decides on treatment — [Withings support](https://support.withings.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000038707-Sleep-Analyzer-EU-ROW-Frequently-asked-questions)
- Trade press framed the Sleep Analyzer as Withings' "strategic shift from wellness products to the health sector", and noted sleep-apnoea tech is notoriously hard to get validated — [Wareable](https://www.wareable.com/health-and-wellbeing/withings-sleep-analyzer-launched-7956)

### Inferences
- Claim ladder for HAVN (derived from Art. 2(1) logic and the precedents above):
  - Safe (environmental/experiential): "quiet, dark, private space to rest", "fresh-air ventilated", "low-emission materials tested to AgBB/REACH", "20-minute power nap", "wind-down lighting".
  - Borderline (general wellness, defensible if not disease-linked and if substantiated): "helps you feel more rested", "designed to support relaxation", "red/amber light setting for evening wind-down", "sound bed for relaxation". Avoid quantified physiological outcomes unless backed by the operator's own study.
  - Out of bounds without MDR: "treats/relieves insomnia, jet lag disorder, burnout, depression", "improves recovery from injury/illness", "lowers blood pressure/cortisol", "anti-ageing/longevity" tied to a physiological mechanism, any sleep-apnoea or "diagnosis" language from embedded sensors.
- Third-party devices (red-light panel, PEMF mat, sound bed) installed in the pod: the device manufacturer's intended purpose governs; HAVN should use only devices whose makers declare "general wellness" (no MDR) and must not add medical claims in its own app/marketing, otherwise HAVN itself could become the "manufacturer" of a medical device by assigning a medical intended purpose. LED red-light panels for skin "treatment" sit next to Annex XVI group 5 (IPL/laser for skin treatment); keep any light feature framed as ambience/circadian lighting, not skin therapy.
- Hospitality framing ("rest", "nap", "sleep lounge") is also what keeps the pod outside the hotel-wellness/medical spa regimes.

### Gaps
- MDR Art. 2(1) definition text, MDCG borderline manual, and the Commission's "Manual on borderline and classification" were not retrievable (EUR-Lex blocked). Definition is cited from memory and should be quoted from the Regulation.
- No sources retrieved for Eight Sleep, Joovv (EU), Philips SmartSleep, Somadome positioning (search budget exhausted). ## Key question 6 (objective e): Hospitality and registration law for overnight pod stays in Germany (Meldeschein, Beherbergungsstätte thresholds, Berlin room-size rules)

### Takeaway
If guests sleep overnight for a fee, the pod operation is a Beherbergungsstätte for registration purposes: under §§29–30 Bundesmeldegesetz the operator must keep a special registration form and have foreign guests sign it by hand on arrival; since 1 January 2025 German nationals no longer have to complete the Meldeschein. Building-law "Beherbergungsstätte" special-building status in Berlin starts above 12 guest beds (BetrVO), and the 2.50 m minimum clear height for Aufenthaltsräume (BauO Bln §47) means a 2.2 m pod cannot itself be an approved guest room — a point to settle with the Bauaufsicht before marketing overnight stays.

### Cited Findings
- §29 BMG: the special registration duty applies to establishments that accommodate guests commercially or on a business basis; the operator must keep a special registration form (Meldeschein); foreign guests must sign it by hand on the day of arrival with the data listed in §30(2) — [lexetius §29 BMG](https://lexetius.com/BMG/29); [buzer §29 BMG](https://www.buzer.de/29_BMG.htm)
- §30(2) BMG limits the form to: date of arrival and expected departure, surname, first names, date of birth, nationalities, address, number of accompanying foreign persons — [lexetius §30 BMG](https://lexetius.com/BMG/30)
- The special registration duty under §§29–30 BMG was abolished for German nationals from 1 January 2025; for guests without German citizenship it continues — [Garmisch-Partenkirchen Tourismus notice](https://gapa-tourismus.de/gapa-tourismus.de/Gastgeberportal/%C3%84nderung%20des%20Melderechts.pdf)

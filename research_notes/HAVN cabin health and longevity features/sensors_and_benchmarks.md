# Smart-room sensing/control layer and competitive benchmarks for self-service sleep cabins

Scope note: Part 1 covers contactless sleep tracking, environmental sensing, smart wake, scene control, reset detection, GDPR and a sleep-report sketch. Part 2 covers comparable pod/capsule/sleep-hotel operators and pod makers. Research method: web search (search-result snippets) only; the sandbox proxy returned 403 on every direct page fetch (ninehours.co.jp, spabusiness.com, withings.com, emfit.com), and the shared web-search budget ran out before the last seven queries. Figures are given in the original currency; euro conversions are my own rough approximations and are flagged. Prices from marketplace listings are indicative, not quotes.

## Key Question 1: Which contactless sleep sensors are accurate enough to give guests a credible sleep score, and what do they cost at 100+ units?

### Takeaway
Under-mattress (Withings) and bedside radar (Google Nest Hub) sensors reach roughly 87% epoch-by-epoch sleep/wake accuracy against polysomnography (PSG) in healthy adults, with weak wake detection (55-73%); this is credible for a "sleep vs. awake, duration, HR, breathing, snoring" report but not for clinical-grade staging. The only PSG-validated, CE/FDA-cleared, off-the-shelf under-mattress product with EU retail availability is the Withings Sleep Analyzer (about EUR 130-150 retail); no 100+ unit pricing was published for any of the devices, and the cheapest credible radar front-ends are Shenzhen 60 GHz modules at about EUR 45-60 per unit at single quantity.

### Cited Findings

Under-mattress ballistocardiography/pneumatic sensors

- Withings Sleep Analyzer (WSA), apnea detection: 118 suspected-apnea patients with simultaneous PSG; sensitivity 88.0% and specificity 88.6% at AHI >= 15/h (AUROC 0.926); at AHI >= 30/h sensitivity 86.0%, specificity 91.2% (AUROC 0.954); registered NCT04234828; several authors are Withings employees — [Edouard et al. 2021, J Clin Sleep Med (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8314651/)
- WSA sleep staging vs. home PSG in 117 healthy adults: sensitivity 93% for sleep, 73% for wake, overall sleep-wake accuracy 87%; authors note stage validation had been lacking; device is CE-marked and FDA-cleared for apnea — [Stefanos et al., medRxiv preprint 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.06.25326860.full.pdf); [JMIR Human Factors 2026](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2026/1/e77033/PDF)
- WSA reported metrics: sleep onset/wake time, apnea episodes with severity class, deep/light/REM cycles, continuous and average heart rate, snoring duration, sleep score — [Medi-shop product listing](https://www.medi-shop.gr/en/sleep-diagnostic-systems/withings-sleep-analyzer)
- WSA EU retail price: recommended retail EUR 149.95 (street EUR 119) in Germany — [tink.de](https://www.tink.de/p/withings-sleep-analyzer-schlafsensormatte); EUR 149.95 in Ireland — [McCabes Pharmacy](https://www.mccabespharmacy.com/products/withings-sleep-analyzer-815180cd27eca462); launch price EUR 129.95 — [iphone-ticker.de](https://www.iphone-ticker.de/withings-sleep-analyzer-neuer-schlaftracker-liegt-unter-der-matratze-156756/); EUR 165.99-179.99 in Baltic Euronics stores — [Euronics EE](https://www.euronics.ee/en/beauty-health/health-care/accessories-and-other/sleepanalyzer/withings-sleep-analyser)
- Emfit QS (Finnish BCG strip) in a sleep lab, 62 patients: data loss in 47% of participants; in the 33 remaining, total sleep time overestimated by 177.5 +/- 119.4 min and WASO underestimated by 44.74 +/- 68.81 min; resting HR accurate; sleep-wake agreement with PSG low — [Kholghi et al. 2022, JCSM (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8974370)
- Emfit QS heart rate vs. ECG at home, 20 healthy adults: mean HR bias about -0.8 bpm (SD 2.3) — [Ranta et al. 2020, JMIR Biomed Eng](https://biomedeng.jmir.org/2020/1/e16620)
- Commercial BCG sensor for apnea screening: sensitivity 0.72, specificity 0.70, R2 0.16 against PSG — [PMC6539222](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6539222/)
- Sleepace RestOn (Shenzhen, brand of Maidijia Technology; founded 2011 by Peking University alumni): 80 cm x 6.4 cm x ~2 mm sensing strap measuring sleep time, HR, respiration rate, body movement and sleep cycles — [TechNode 2014](https://technode.com/2014/11/24/reston-sleep-tracker/); [WEF profile](https://www.weforum.org/organizations/sleepace/); teardown reports a piezoelectric film sensor attributed to TE Connectivity (single secondary source) — [AllPCB teardown](https://www.allpcb.com/allelectrohub/teardown-reston-portable-non-wearable-sleep-monitor)

Radar / mmWave sensors

- Google Nest Hub (2nd gen, Soli 60 GHz) vs. PSG in 33 healthy sleepers aged 19-78, scored by two sleep technologists: epoch-by-epoch sleep-wake accuracy 87%, 96% of sleep epochs and 55% of wake epochs detected; "not intended for medical or diagnostic use"; placement, nearby people/pets and noise can cause errors — [Google Research blog](https://www.research.google/blog/contactless-sleep-sensing-in-nest-hub/); [Google paper](https://research.google/pubs/sleep-wake-detection-with-a-contactless-bedside-radar-sleep-sensing-system/); [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/smart-speakers/google-nest-hub-review/)
- mmWave radar vs. PSG for sleep apnea in 68 adults: sensitivity 92.7%, specificity 84.6%, kappa 0.731 — [Med Sci Monit 2025](https://medscimonit.com/abstract/full/idArt/948079)
- Pediatric mmWave radar (QSA600) vs. PSG in 281 children, Beijing, late 2023: AHI agreement ICC 0.945 (95% CI 0.93-0.96); staging accuracy not reported in the abstract listing — [CatalyzeX author listing](https://catalyzex.com/author/Zhifei%20Xu)
- UWB radar four-class staging (wake/REM/light/deep) in 47 older adults: accuracy 79.5%, kappa 0.65 — [arXiv 2601.04057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04057v1)
- Sleepal AI Lamp (contactless radar lamp) staging model validated on 1,022 PSG-annotated nights; authors note most prior studies have small datasets or lack large-scale PSG labels — [arXiv 2604.16442](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.16442); launch coverage is sponsored content — [Sleep Review](https://sleepreviewmag.com/sleep-diagnostics/connected-care/ai-machine-learning/contactless-sleep-monitoring-moves-toward-validation-standards-what-the-sleepal-ai-lamp-launch-signals-for-home-sleep-assessment/)
- Changsha Tsingray Technology registered a clinical trial (Sept 2023) of a mmWave radar sleep respiratory monitoring system vs. PSG for staging and sleep-breathing disorders; no results posted — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06038006](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06038006)
- Infineon XENSIV BGT60UTR11AIP (July 2023): 16 mm2 60 GHz FMCW radar with antennas in package, vital-sign (heart/respiration) detection with sub-mm motion sensitivity, hardware deep-sleep mode; targeted at sleep trackers and baby monitors; DEMO board and Radar Development Kit available — [Infineon press release](https://infineon.com/cms/en/about-infineon/press/market-news/2023/INFPSS202307-136.html); [EE Journal](https://www.eejournal.com/industry_news/infineon-presents-highly-integrated-xensiv-60-ghz-radar-sensor-for-consumer-electronics-iot-applications-and-healthcare-devices)

Operator-grade contactless tracking already deployed in capsules

- Nine Hours (Japan) "9h sleep fitscan": sensors in the capsule bed measure body movement, snoring sounds and sleep facial images with guest consent; report sent to guest; guests with poor metrics are referred to university hospitals and clinics — [Nine Hours sleep analysis page](https://ninehours.co.jp/en/sleep-analysis-report); [Nine Hours sleep business](https://ninehours.co.jp/en/business/service/sleep-business)

### Inferences
- For a hospitality "sleep score", the defensible claim set is: time in bed, total sleep time, sleep/wake timeline, HR trend, respiration rate, snoring minutes, movement index. Stage labels (deep/REM) should be shown as "estimated" because even the best contactless devices have 55-73% wake sensitivity and staging kappa around 0.65 at best.
- Emfit QS is unsuitable as the primary cabin sensor (47% data loss, +3 h TST error in a lab population); it remains acceptable for HR/HRV trend only.
- A Guangzhou manufacturer has two realistic architectures: (a) buy finished Withings Sleep Analyzers (about EUR 120-150 each at retail; CE/FDA-cleared, PSG-validated, but a closed consumer app/cloud and Wi-Fi only), or (b) integrate a 60 GHz radar module (Seeed/DFRobot/Hi-Link class, EUR 45-60 single unit, likely well under EUR 30 at volume given a CNY 59 radar night light exists) with its own staging algorithm, which then needs its own validation.
- Radar modules that report "bed occupancy" and "sleep status" through bedding (R60ABD1-class) double as reset/occupancy sensors, saving a separate pressure mat.
- Withings-class under-mattress sensors are single-sleeper devices; the cabin's single 80-90 cm bed is an advantage here (no bed-sharing confound).

### Gaps
- No published 100+ unit / B2B price for Withings Sleep Analyzer, Emfit QS, Sleepace or Aqara FP2; Withings Health Solutions / API terms were not retrievable (withings.com returned 403 via the sandbox proxy, and the search budget ended before the Emfit/Sleepace B2B queries ran).
- No unit price found for the Infineon BGT60UTR11AIP chip or for R60ABD1-class Shenzhen sleep radar modules.
- No independent (non-manufacturer) PSG validation found for Aqara FP2, Sleepace RestOn, or any Alibaba-sourced sleep radar module; staging accuracy of the pediatric QSA600 radar study was not visible in the abstract.
- Withings' own sleep-score formula is not published in the sources found.

## Key Question 2: What is the state of "smart wake" and closed-loop environment control in commercial sleep products?

### Takeaway
"Wake in light sleep" is widely marketed but the best controlled trial (2024) found little objective benefit from a multimodal light/sound/temperature smart alarm, with effects depending on chronotype and on light exposure lasting more than 15 minutes; dawn-simulation and red-light-through-closed-eyes findings are the most promising wake mechanisms. Closed-loop scene control is mature commodity technology (Zigbee/Tuya hotel RCUs, Matter air-quality device types, I2C sensor modules at EUR 15-35), but off-the-shelf products do not yet close the loop from a sleep sensor to the environment; Equinox Hotel's "30 minutes before sleep the room transitions" is the most advanced hospitality example found.

### Cited Findings

Smart wake / sleep inertia

- Campanella et al. 2024 (Clocks & Sleep): four lab nights, waking by conventional alarm vs. a multimodal bedroom device combining light, sound and temperature; "little overall impact of the intervention", but chronotype and the length of light exposure both influenced sleep-inertia symptoms; evening types did better with light exposure longer than 15 minutes; authors suggest intervention duration matters more than the device — [PMC10969141](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10969141/); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-5175/6/1/13)
- Sleep inertia is most pronounced when waking from slow-wave sleep (Hilditch & McHill 2019, as cited in the above); an alarm clock with dawn simulation significantly reduced complaints of sleep inertia; saturated red light delivered through closed eyes mitigated sleep inertia on waking — [PMC10969141 (background and discussion)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10969141/)
- Consumer stage-timed alarms depend on stage detection whose sensitivity vs. PSG is often 50-86%; no PSG-validated home trial of a stage-timed benefit was found; vendor blogs claiming a 2024 study "confirmed" light-stage waking benefits contradict the trial's objective findings — [RISE blog (vendor)](https://www.risescience.com/blog/smart-alarm); [Mattress Miracle (affiliate)](https://mattressmiracle.ca/blogs/mattress-miracle-blog/best-smart-wake-up)

Closed-loop / scene control in commercial sleep rooms

- Equinox Hotel New York: rooms built on a "dark, quiet and cool" principle with light-absorbent and sound-dampening finishes, blackout curtains and HVAC with smart thermostats; adaptive mattress with personalised temperature control and dual-zone settings; 30 minutes before sleep the room transitions to the sleep environment and a soundscape eases guests awake — [Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/crafting-a-luxury-sleep-experience-at-the-equinox-hotel-new-york); one-button "quiet, dark, cool" setting — [SmarterTravel](https://www.smartertravel.com/hotels-with-the-best-sleep-amenities/)
- Napcabs (Munich): touchscreen control of lighting, temperature and alarm inside the cabin (2012 description) — [Executive Traveller](https://www.executivetraveller.com/napcab-sleeping-working-cabins-your-own-mini-lounge-in-munich)
- Zedwell Piccadilly capsules: individual ventilation, climate control, noise reduction, ambient lighting per capsule — [Globetrender 2025](https://globetrender.com/2025/10/14/zedwell-capsule-hotel-opens-in-london/)
- First Cabin bedside panel: reading light, emergency button, fan switch, earphone jack, USB and power outlet — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20150308-first-cabin-compact-hotel)

Environmental sensors and modules (prices as listed)

- OXmaint: combine door-lock activity with motion/thermostat sensors; housekeeping dispatch triggered on confirmed departure (marketing benchmarks) — [OXmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/hospitality/smart-occupancy-analytics-hotel-operations)

- Radar sleep modules report bed occupancy directly (R60ABD1) — [Tindie](https://www.tindie.com/products/fuxinsuo/60ghz-mmwave-sleep-monitoring-radar-module/)

### Inferences
- A credible cabin controller BOM for a Guangzhou builder: ESP32-C6/Zigbee or Tuya Zigbee gateway + DIN-rail RCU for lights/fan/heater; SCD41 (CO2/T/RH, ~USD 15-20 at volume) + SPS30 (PM, ~USD 33 at 100) + an SGP4x VOC chip (price not found) on I2C to a local MCU; a 60 GHz radar module for presence, breathing and bed occupancy; an I2S MEMS microphone for snoring/noise dB; an ambient-light sensor. Modbus/RS485 is not native to these chips and would need a converter board, so I2C-to-MCU-to-Zigbee/Matter is the natural path.
- Closed-loop control that is actually evidence-backed today: (1) pre-sleep dimming and cooling (Equinox pattern), (2) CO2-driven ventilation, (3) a dawn-simulation or red-light wake ramp of at least 15 minutes. "Wake in light sleep" should be offered as a soft window (e.g. up to 20 minutes early) rather than a headline health claim.
- Reset detection can be done with the same radar (no person, no breathing signal) plus a door contact and a weight/pressure change on the mattress; "bed-made" detection specifically was not found in any product and would likely require a camera, which conflicts with privacy positioning — a staff-confirmed checklist on the cleaning app is the pragmatic alternative.
- Matter 1.3 already has an Air Quality Sensor device type (0x002C) in certified products, so a Matter-over-Thread sensor node would be interoperable with mainstream controllers; Tuya Zigbee remains the cheaper Chinese-supply-chain route.

### Gaps
- No Tuya/Zigbee product with a documented PMS (property management system) connector was found; integration with the booking/access system would be custom via the gateway's cloud API or MQTT.
- No price found for Sensirion SGP41 VOC or for Bosch BME688, and no Modbus/RS485 variants of these modules were found.
- No commercial pod or hotel product was found that closes the loop from a live sleep-stage signal to lighting/temperature; Equinox's sequence appears time-scheduled, not sensor-driven (the source does not say).
- The Matter 1.3 specification text itself was not retrieved; device-type coverage is inferred from certified product listings.

Only Nine Hours sells (bundles) in-capsule sleep measurement with a report.

## Key Question 6: Privacy and GDPR constraints on health data in the EU (sleep and vital-sign data)

### Takeaway
Sleep stages, heart rate, respiration and snoring collected in a cabin are "data concerning health" under GDPR Art. 4(15)/Art. 9: the 2015 Article 29 Working Party annex already treated raw sensor data from which health conclusions can be drawn as health data, and the CJEU's October 2024 Lindenapotheke ruling (C-21/23) confirmed a broad reading where neither the accuracy of the inference nor the controller's intent matters. Practically this means explicit opt-in consent per stay, a lawful basis and DPIA, anonymisation for any research/benchmark use, and no camera-based sensing unless strictly justified.

### Cited Findings
- CJEU C-21/23 (Lindenapotheke, Oct 2024): information customers provide when buying pharmacy-only non-prescription medicines online is "data concerning health" (Art. 4(15)); the concept must be interpreted broadly; it is immaterial whether the inferred information is accurate or whether the controller intended to obtain special-category data; the Court went beyond the Advocate General, who thought the inference too tenuous — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2024/global/feeling-unwell-after-the-cjeus-lindenapotheke-decision); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/zh-hant/insights-and-events/insights/2024/10/ecj-lindenapotheke); [MediaLaws comment](https://www.medialaws.eu/comment-on-cjeu-c-21-23-lindenapotheke/); competitors may sue for GDPR breaches under unfair-competition law — [Mason Hayes & Curran](https://www.mhc.ie/latest/insights/prescription-medicines-and-the-gdpr)
- Article 29 Working Party annex (5 Feb 2015) on lifestyle/wellbeing apps: data is health data in three cases — clearly medical data; raw sensor data that can support health conclusions alone or combined with other data; and data from which health conclusions can be drawn, whether or not accurate; profiling/marketing uses flagged as the main risk — [Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/article-29-working-party-clarifies-scope-health-data-processed-lifestyle-wellbeing-apps); [Hunton PDF of the annex](https://huntonak.com/media/legal/1944_clarifications-scope-health-data-via-lifestyle-apps.pdf); [Covington](https://www.covingtonblogs.com/2015/04/22/article-29-working-party-clarifies-health-data/)
- EDPB Guidelines 03/2020 (health data in research): all processing of health data must comply with Art. 5 principles and one legal ground under Art. 6 plus an Art. 9 derogation — [Burges Salmon summary](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102g6da/balancing-privacy-and-scientific-research-edpb-publishes-guidelines-on-the-use-o)
- Commentator view (unverified attribution to an "EDPB Opinion 04/2022"): combining sleep quality, HRV, resting HR and activity into a wellness score generates health data at the output level — [Thryve blog](https://www.thryve.health/blog/wearable-data-special-category-gdpr) (the cited EDPB opinion could not be confirmed)
- Operator precedents: Nine Hours requires guest consent, anonymises data and refers at-risk guests to hospitals — [ninehours.co.jp](https://ninehours.co.jp/en/sleep-analysis-report); Sleepiz/Infineon radar collects vital signs "anonymously without identifying the person" — [IoT M2M Council](https://iotm2mcouncil.org/iot-library/news/connected-health-news/infineon-radar-sensor-targets-medical-devices/); Applied Sensor Co. markets hotel presence sensing with "no cameras or microphones" — [ASC](https://asc.com/applications/hospitality)
- Google states Nest Hub Sleep Sensing is "not intended for medical or diagnostic use" — [Google Research](https://www.research.google/blog/contactless-sleep-sensing-in-nest-hub/)

### Inferences
- Design implications for an EU cabin network: (1) sleep tracking off by default, switched on per stay by explicit Art. 9(2)(a) consent in the app, with a plain-language description of HR/respiration/snoring capture; (2) no facial imaging or IR cameras (Nine Hours' Japanese practice would be hard to justify under necessity/minimisation in the EU); (3) microphone processing on-device for snoring minutes and dB only, no audio retention; (4) pseudonymised stay IDs, short retention, EU hosting; (5) a DPIA before the Berlin pilot (systematic monitoring of special-category data); (6) any "health risk" messaging (e.g. apnea suspicion) should be phrased as a prompt to see a doctor, mirroring Nine Hours, since the cabin product is not a medical device.
- Using a closed consumer device (Withings) makes the operator a controller and Withings a processor/independent controller; a data-processing agreement and the device's cloud location become part of the compliance file.

### Gaps
- No EDPB guideline or national DPA (e.g. German DSK, CNIL) document specifically addressing in-room sleep sensors or wellness sleep scores was found; the EDPB's own site was not reachable through the sandbox.
- The 2015 WP29 annex pre-dates GDPR; its continued authority is assumed, not verified by a post-2018 EDPB endorsement in the sources found.

## Key Question 7: Sketch of a "sleep report" a guest could receive after a stay

### Takeaway
Existing precedents (Nine Hours email report with snoring count; Withings app with cycles, HR, snoring duration, apnea class and score; NTT Data's AI report with trends and improvement suggestions) support a one-page report with a timeline, five or six measured metrics, environment context and one or two suggestions; stage labels should be marked as estimates and any apnea-like finding routed to "see a doctor", not diagnosed.

### Cited Findings
- Nine Hours report: body movement, snoring, breathing patterns, sent to the guest; hospital referral when metrics are poor — [ninehours.co.jp](https://ninehours.co.jp/en/sleep-analysis-report); reports arrive by email after checkout and require about 6-7 hours of sleep — [Mapping the Plot](https://mappingtheplot.substack.com/p/modern-convenience-9h-nine-hours); report tells guests how many times they snored — [SoraNews24](https://soranews24.com/2022/05/19/this-japanese-capsule-hotel-tells-you-how-many-times-you-snore-during-the-night/amp/)
- NTT Data Sleep Lab report: sleep onset/wake/turning, heart rate, breathing, snoring; AI analysis returned as a report with sleep trends and suggestions for improvement; referral to cooperating hospitals if e.g. sleep apnea is suspected — [JREF/NTT Data coverage](https://jref.com/threads/ntt-data-capsule-hotel-to-monitor-guests%E2%80%99-sleep.603666/)
- Withings report content: sleep onset and wake time, apnea episodes with severity class, deep/light/REM cycles, continuous and average HR, snoring duration, sleep score — [Medi-shop listing](https://www.medi-shop.gr/en/sleep-diagnostic-systems/withings-sleep-analyzer)
- Aqara FP2 sleep mode outputs: sleep duration, sleep stages, heart rate, respiratory rate — [MightyGadget](https://mightygadget.co.uk/aqara-presence-sensor-fp2-sleep-monitoring/)
- Google's PSG comparison shows wake epochs are the least reliable (55%) — [Google Research](https://www.research.google/blog/contactless-sleep-sensing-in-nest-hub/)
- Equinox frames the environment as "dark, quiet, cool" with smart thermostats — [Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/crafting-a-luxury-sleep-experience-at-the-equinox-hotel-new-york)

### Inferences
Proposed report structure (my synthesis of the above, suited to 2 h to overnight stays):
1. Header: stay type (nap / overnight / Health Pod hour), cabin, date, local time zone.
2. Timeline strip: in bed, estimated asleep, awakenings, out of bed; for naps under 90 min show only "time to settle" and "rest duration".
3. Measured metrics (label "measured"): time in bed, estimated total sleep, time to fall asleep, number/length of awakenings, average and lowest resting HR, average respiration rate, snoring minutes and loudest dB(A), movement index.
4. Estimated metrics (label "estimated, not a medical measurement"): light/deep/REM minutes; sleep score 0-100 built from duration, continuity, time to fall asleep and HR recovery.
5. Environment during the stay (from the cabin sensors): CO2 range, temperature, humidity, noise dB(A) at the pillow, light level at wake; whether the pre-sleep dim/cool scene and the wake ramp were used.

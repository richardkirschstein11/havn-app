# Virtual windows and virtual skylights for a windowless prefab sleeping cabin (2.0 x 2.4 x 2.2 m, 100 mm wall panels, Berlin pilot Nov 2026, up to 2,500 units in 5 years)

Research method note: ~45 web searches (standard + extended) were run on 2026-10-21. Direct page fetches (manufacturer PDFs, ScienceDirect abstracts) were blocked by the session's egress proxy, and the shared web-search budget ran out before a final verification batch, so some figures below rest on search-engine summaries of the linked pages rather than on the pages themselves. Those are marked "(search summary)". Nothing has been invented; where a number could not be sourced it is listed under Gaps.

## KQ1. Which products exist, and what are their technology, sizes, depth, light output, CCT, power, price and target markets?

### Takeaway
Four technology families are purchasable in 2026: (1) Rayleigh-scattering "sun + sky" units (CoeLux, Innerscene A7/Virtual Sun, Mitsubishi Misola, Yeelight P21, Chinese OEM clones) which need 120–465 mm of depth and 80–200 W; (2) thin tunable-white "sky" LED panels without a sun (Innerscene Circadian Sky at 84 mm deep, 35–75 W; Sky Factory Luminous SkyCeilings/Virtual Windows at 46 mm; Chinese "blue sky" panels at roughly $60–600); (3) display-based "digital windows" (LiquidView at $3k–50k needing 150–200 mm wall depth, Atmoph Window 2 at ~$380–448 for a 27" FHD screen); (4) backlit photographic light boxes. No public list price exists for CoeLux, Innerscene or Mitsubishi; dealer listings put CoeLux HT25 at ~$15.6–17.4k, Misola at ~$6–7k, and Innerscene says 5–6 A7 units cost about one CoeLux LC45 (~$29k at 2015 prices).

### Cited Findings

#### Technology taxonomy
- Rayleigh-scattering skylights put a polymer screen loaded with titanium-dioxide nanoparticles in front of a white LED, so that blue light is scattered (sky) while the direct beam stays warm (sun) — [Core77](https://core77.com/posts/26936/New-Light-Panel-Technology-Imagine-Having-a-Ceiling-Skylight-on-the-1st-Floor-of-a-Multistory-Building)
- A second architecture uses "a broadband, spot-like light source and a Rayleigh scattering panel placed at a certain distance from the source" — the distance is what creates the depth and the sun; this is the geometry claimed in a 2024-granted US patent "Sun-sky-imitating illumination device" — [Google Patents US12000583B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12000583)
- Chinese trade press (2020) states the core Rayleigh optical sheet was for years produced and sold exclusively by Italy's CoeLux, and lists domestic entrants: Changzhou Fengsheng (FS Optronics), Opple, Yeelight, Pak (三雄极光) and Shenzhen Yichuang (search summary) — [OFweek Lighting](https://lighting.ofweek.com/2020-03/ART-34001-8210-30433175.html); FS Optronics markets a "Rayleigh-effect sky lamp" — [FS Optronics](https://www.fsoptronics.com/new_detail/nid/43655.html)
- A DIY guide for building a low-cost Rayleigh sky lamp circulates in China, indicating the optical sheet is now commoditised — [SMZDM DIY post](https://post.smzdm.com/p/an3e34k7/)
- Mitsubishi's Misola uses the same Rayleigh principle: "a slim sky-blue panel and a four-sided frame" — [Mitsubishi Electric Asia](https://sg.mitsubishielectric.com/en/our-stories/article/xx_ww_g_in2004_02.html)
- Backlit photographic sky ceilings (Sky Factory) use "translucent images illuminated from behind at a calibrated light intensity and frequency (color temperature)" and fit standard ceiling grids — [Sky Factory guide spec](https://skyfactory.com/guidespec_tsf_lsc_071019)
- Display "windows" (Sky Factory eScape) embed "an industrial-grade HD LCD monitor in a full-size casement window frame", content shot on RED cinema cameras in unedited 30-min-to-hours sequences — [Sky Factory Japan eNews 2010](https://www.japan.skyfactory.com/eNews/archive/2010/june/)
- Head-tracked parallax "windows" (webcam tracks viewer, scene re-projected) exist as open-source/academic demos; the illusion "only works for one user at a time" — [Stanford CS231A project](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/prev_projects_2016/final-project.pdf); [desktop-vr on GitHub](https://github.com/jasondecamp/desktop-vr)

### Inferences
- Rayleigh sun+sky products cluster at 190–270 mm depth (Yeelight 190, CoeLux HT25 250, Innerscene A7 254–267) because the sun illusion needs source-to-diffuser distance; the LS Matte needed 465 mm. The ~120 mm Misola achieves a shallower build by dropping the parallel "sun" beam and faking shadow with an asymmetrically lit frame.
- At roughly 5,600–5,800 lm from 200 W (28–29 lm/W), CoeLux HT25 and Innerscene A7 are 3–4x less efficient than Circadian Sky (53–91 lm/W) or ordinary LED panels, because most energy is lost in the scattering optics; for a sealed 10.6 m³ cabin this is also 200 W of heat.
- Price tiers (unit, ex-install): Chinese OEM Rayleigh/blue-sky panel $60–600; Yeelight P21 ~€370; Atmoph $380–450; Innerscene Circadian Sky likely low-thousands (unverified); Misola ~$6–7k; Innerscene A7 ~$5–6k by the "5–6 per LC45" rule of thumb (inference); CoeLux HT25 $15.6–17.4k; LiquidView $3k–50k.

## KQ2. Which approach gives the strongest "window" illusion in a very shallow cabin wall (~100 mm) or ceiling, and how does it integrate (ceiling vs wall, heat, controls, sunrise)?

### Takeaway
Within 100 mm of wall depth only three options fit without a bump-out: Innerscene Circadian Sky (84 mm, tunable sky without sun), Sky Factory's 46 mm edge-lit Luminous Virtual Window/SkyCeiling light boxes, and thin LCD/OLED "digital window" screens; every true sun+sky Rayleigh unit needs 190–270 mm and therefore has to sit above the cabin roof (feasible for a room-in-room cabin if the host building's ceiling allows) or in a wall bump-out.

- Innerscene Circadian Sky needs only 84 mm (3-5/16 in) plenum, "mounts flush to any interior wall", ships in 300x600 to 600x1200 mm sizes, and draws ~35–75 W — [Innerscene FAQ](https://www.innerscene.com/faq/circadian-sky); [Innerscene Circadian Sky](https://www.innerscene.com/products/circadian-sky)

- Motion parallax from head movement is a major contributor to believability of a simulated view; without it scenes feel "disembodied or less believable" — [Journal of Perceptual Imaging](https://library.imaging.org/jpi/articles/3/2/jpi0129)

### Inferences
- For a 100 mm wall panel, the realistic shortlist is: (a) Circadian Sky 450x1200 or 600x1200 flush in the wall (84 mm, plus driver routed into the panel cavity); (b) a Sky Factory-style 46 mm edge-lit light box with a custom sky/landscape image (cheapest, static, could be OEM-made in Guangzhou); (c) a 27–43" thin LCD/OLED screen behind a window frame (Atmoph-class, ~30–60 mm) showing slow nature loops. A sun+sky Rayleigh unit is only possible in the ceiling with the housing protruding ~200–270 mm above the cabin roof, which is acceptable for a room-in-room cabin provided the host building has >2.5 m clear height and the roof panel is cut to a 1070x520 (HT25) or ~970x530 (A7) opening.
- Because the cabin is for sleeping, the window must also function as a dawn simulator: the Rayleigh units and Circadian Sky have the CCT range (≤2,500 K at dawn) and dimming to do this; a photographic light box only if driven by a tunable-white edge-lit driver; an LCD screen can show a sunrise video but delivers far less vertical illuminance at the pillow than a ceiling skylight.
- Heat: a 200 W HT25/A7 at full sun equals roughly the heat of two adults in a 10.6 m³ sealed box; the 35–75 W Circadian Sky or a 20–40 W light box is far easier for a cabin ventilation system to carry (inference from wattages above; no thermal study found).

### Gaps
- No study was found that compares illusion strength (perceived "realness") across Rayleigh skylight vs tunable sky panel vs LCD window in the same room; the A7-vs-CoeLux comparison page is vendor-authored.
- Innerscene Circadian Sky driver location/size (whether the 84 mm figure includes the driver) was not confirmed.
- No vendor documents wall-mounted (vertical) performance of the Rayleigh sun units; Misola is the only confirmed wall-embedded Rayleigh product.
- Vertical illuminance at the pillow (lux at 1–2 m) was not published for any product; dawn-simulation efficacy depends on it.

## KQ3. What does the research say about virtual windows/skylights (mood, anxiety, perceived room size, circadian rhythm, sleep, patient outcomes)?

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence (mostly 2017–2025, small lab samples of 10–100 people) consistently shows artificial skylights and digital windows improve mood, reduce tension/anxiety/claustrophobia and increase relaxation, with weaker and inconsistent effects on cognition, productivity, physiology and perceived spaciousness; the only larger clinical dataset (n=181 inpatients, 53% less acute stress) is vendor-reported, and no published ICU, submarine or offshore trial of a virtual window exists — a registered Turkish ICU RCT never reported results.

### Cited Findings

#### Laboratory and office studies of artificial skylights/windows
- Canazei et al. 2017, Building and Environment 124:69–77: randomized controlled lab study, n=100 healthy young adults, windowless office lit by fluorescent lamps vs a Rayleigh artificial skylight (cloudless sky + sun); skylight group reported lower tension, anxiety and claustrophobic symptoms, higher positive mood, felt more connected to nature, rated the room more "lively", less "tense" and "detached" — but also made riskier and more selfish decisions (pre-2020 evidence) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013231730344X); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318842898_Artificial_skylight_effects_in_a_windowless_office_environment)
- Yasukouchi et al. 2019, J Physiological Anthropology 38:10: n=10 healthy men, simulated office under Rayleigh skylight vs fluorescent; nocturnal melatonin secretion was significantly greater after daytime skylight exposure; authors conclude skylights maintain work performance "with less psychological and physiological stress" (pre-2020, very small n) — [BMC](https://jphysiolanthropol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40101-019-0203-4); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31462321/)
- Fregna, Lucchi & Prestifilippo 2024, "Artificial skylight improves sleep quality and emotional state in windowless offices: A randomized controlled study" — cited by CoeLux as showing calmer participants and higher night-time melatonin; journal and sample size could not be verified (vendor-relayed) — [CoeLux office page](https://www.coelux.com/en/news/office-artificial-skylight-benefits-14533); [CoeLux healthcare page](https://www.coelux.com/en/news/healing-with-light-coelux-healthcare-14750)
- A 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of light and wellbeing notes artificial skylights lowered heart rate, raised HRV and self-reported relaxation, but found no effect on mood or cognitive outcomes (search summary of supplementary material) — [J Happiness Studies 2024 supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs10902-024-00838-4/MediaObjects/10902_2024_838_MOESM1_ESM.docx)
- 2025 "Reviving windowless spaces: exploring fatigue relief and emotional benefits of artificial windows" (Journal of Building Engineering): randomized crossover, n=32, three identical rooms (real window, windowless, tunable artificial window), 15-min seated rest; artificial window reduced visual fatigue and supported emotional and physiological recovery; positive-affect score rose 73% under the artificial window — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352710225015669)
- 2024 "Comparative study on human responses in isolated and confined offices with an artificial window" (Building and Environment): artificial windows "are not different from the real in mood and subjective alertness"; real windows retained advantages in physiological measures and restorative perception; "over lighting at eye level may hinder arousal, cognitive performances and mood restoration" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132324008588)
- 2025 follow-up on simulated view content in the same isolated-office setup: significant mood restoration and restorative perception, no significant physiological restoration; dynamic, natural, well-rendered content performed best — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132325004044)
- 2023 simulated-underground-space study (Energy and Buildings): no significant differences between artificial and real windows on cognitive performance (with views), physiology (without views), mood or alertness; the artificial window's view "did not show benefits in physiological parameters, restoration, or lighting perceptions" — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778823006709)
- 2018 sub-basement study of indoor plants and artificial windows: positive emotions higher and negative emotions lower with the (artificial) window than windowless (pre-2020) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132318302403)
- Wohn et al. 2020, ACM IMX, "A Room With a 'Fake' View": between-subjects field experiment installing a nature-video digital window in windowless offices; mood and happiness rose, productivity change not significant (sample size not retrievable) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3391614.3399397); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342256033_A_Room_With_a_Fake_View_Installing_Digital_Windows_in_Windowless_Offices)
- Earlier Friedman et al. "office window of the future" (live outdoor footage on a large display; increased social connection and psychological wellbeing; pre-2020) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220107819_Office_window_of_the_future-Field-based_analyses_of_a_new_use_of_a_large_display)
- Li, Tian, Zhang & Zou 2024 review (Building and Environment 259): frames windowless workers as experiencing "negative emotion and a high level of stress", reviews artificial-window studies on mood and productivity, and criticises experimental designs/evaluation methods (full conclusions not retrievable) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132324004979)
- A 2026 systematic review (in press, Energy and Buildings) screened 47 studies of multi-domain interventions (artificial windows, skylights, lighting, greenery) for restoration in windowless environments — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378778826012958)
- Real-window benchmark: UC Berkeley randomized crossover, n=86, with vs without windows: working memory and concentration improved with windows; short-term memory, planning and creativity unaffected — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132320301372); [CBE summary](https://cbe.berkeley.edu/research/impact-of-window-views-on-comfort-emotions-cognitive-performance/)

#### Hospital / patient studies
- Pati et al., HERD Oct 2015 (reported by vendor): n=181 med-surg inpatients at Covenant Health, Lubbock TX, identical rooms differing only by a Luminous SkyCeiling; acute stress 53.40% lower and anxiety 34.79% lower; single-blind (pre-2020; vendor-relayed, conflict of interest) — [Sky Factory Japan eNews](https://www.japan.skyfactory.com/eNews/archive/2015/october/); vendor narrative — [The Big Illusion PDF](https://skyfactory.com/app/uploads/2023/08/The-Big-Illusion.pdf)
- A 2025 Building Research & Information paper cites prior work that ceiling panels showing day/night sky during radiotherapy and sky ceilings in patient rooms "reduced acute stress and anxiety", and a CT-room study where projected moving nature scenes (hill, grass, slowly moving clouds) indirectly reduced anxiety via perceived pleasantness — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09613218.2025.2603581)
- Panasonic reports that Fukui University Hospital measured brain waves, pulse and saliva with its +WINDOW and found "a relaxing effect in windowless rooms" (vendor only, no n or statistics) — [Panasonic FLF](https://panasonic.net/design/flf/works/window/); a separate Panasonic/Univ. of Fukui pseudo-skylight + projector trial ran Oct 2019–Jan 2020 in a hospital waiting room — [re-how.net](https://re-how.net/all/649258/)
- LiquidView claims electrodermal activity dropped 46.5% on average when participants viewed its windows in a windowless Stanford lab (vendor claim, unpublished) — [AVIXA](https://www.avixa.org/explore/articles/digital-windows-mental-health)
- ICU with real windows: a Turkish prospective study found night-time delirium observation scores differed significantly between ICU patients with and without a window — [Turkish J Intensive Care (Bulut & Bahadır Yılmaz)](https://turkishjic.org/article/view/672); Kohn et al. "impact of windows on outcomes of medical ICU patients" exists but details were not retrievable — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318442479_The_impact_of_windows_on_the_outcomes_of_medical_intensive_care_unit_patients)
- A randomized trial "The Effect of Digital Window on Day and Night Perception Status and Sleep Quality in Intensive Care Patients" (Ankara Yıldırım Beyazıt University) was registered but last verified Aug 2022 with status unknown/"not yet recruiting" and no results — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05486780](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05486780); a newer registration "Digital Stimulation and Anxiety and Sleep Quality in ICU Patients" exists — [NCT07820839](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07820839)
- For context, VR headsets (not windows) reduce in-hospital pain and anxiety across 37 studies, n=3,152 (2025 meta-analysis) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589719/)

#### Isolated/confined environments (submarine, Antarctic, space analogs)
- An experiment at the Bulgarian Antarctic base tested personalized projected natural scenery: negative emotions and anxiety still rose and positive emotions fell over time for all participants, but group differences suggested projected nature "can be effective in reducing negative mental states in solitary confinement" (search summary; mapping to link uncertain) — [Frontiers in Physiology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1700518/full); related: natural-scene VR as a countermeasure in three ICE analog case studies — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/360799497_Natural_Scene_Virtual_Reality_as_a_Behavioral_Health_Countermeasure_in_Isolated_Confined_and_Extreme_Environments_Three_Isolated_Confined_Extreme_Analog_Case_Studies)
- A 14-day chamber study found mood and sleep disturbances under isolation and crowding; VR nature was less appealing to participants with strong social support — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9705780/)
- ESA's SubSea project uses 60-day Portuguese Navy submarine patrols (25 volunteers) to study stress, mood and cortisol; no artificial-window intervention is described — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Submarines_for_space_exploration)

#### Perceived spaciousness
- An immersive-VR study (n=14) comparing an outdoor view with frosted glass, a painting and no window found no significant differences in spaciousness ratings, though a distant vista made people underestimate the distance to the far wall — [UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/exploring-the-use-of-immersive-virtual-reality-to-assess-the-impa/)
- A personalised-workspace study reported that projecting a natural landscape on an artificial window improved the space impression (search summary) — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/978-3-030-31908-3_21)
- Full-scale window-form studies: a single large horizontal window at mid-height makes a room appear "very wide and very low" (pre-2020) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233461691_The_Impact_of_Window_Form_on_the_Size_Impression_of_the_Room-Full-Scale_Studies)
- Canazei 2017 participants rated the windowless room more "lively", less "tense"/"detached" under the skylight (spaciousness not directly measured) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013231730344X)

### Inferences
- The effect that is robust across independent studies is affective (tension, anxiety, claustrophobia, positive affect) and, for Rayleigh skylights, physiological calming (HR/HRV) — exactly the outcomes a sleeping/recovery cabin targets. Cognitive/productivity claims are not supported and should not be used in marketing.
- Daytime exposure to a bright sky-like skylight raised night-time melatonin in a tiny study and is claimed to improve sleep in the (unverified) 2024 RCT; this supports using the window as a daytime "light dose" device rather than a night-time feature.
- The two best-designed recent studies (2024 and 2025 isolated-office series) warn that bright light at eye level can backfire; a wall-mounted bright sky panel facing the bed is therefore riskier than a ceiling or high-wall placement.
- There is no clinical evidence specific to sleep quality in windowless sleeping rooms with a virtual window; any HAVN claim here would be extrapolation and is a candidate for an in-house pilot study.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve sample sizes/effect sizes for the 2024 Fregna RCT, Wohn 2020, the 2024 ICE-office study or the Li 2024 review conclusions (abstract pages blocked).
- No peer-reviewed submarine, offshore-platform or Antarctic study of a built-in artificial window (as opposed to projections/VR) was found.
- No independent replication of the n=181 Pati inpatient study was found; it appears only via the vendor.
- No study measured perceived room size with a Rayleigh skylight or a tunable sky panel specifically.

## KQ4. Risks and failure modes (uncanny effect, light leakage during sleep, heat, flicker, blue light at night) and EU compliance; how do products mitigate them?

### Takeaway
Documented risks are behavioural (riskier/more selfish decisions under artificial sun), over-lighting at eye level, loop repetition and single-viewer parallax breakdown for screens, 80–200 W heat loads, and night-time blue light from "night sky" modes; mitigations in current products are scheduling (dawn/sunset/night scenes), very wide CCT ranges (down to 1,600–2,500 K) and deep dimming, while EU compliance hinges on CE (LVD/EMC), EN 62471 risk-group labelling (RG0/RG1 needs no measures) and, for mains LED sources, Ecodesign flicker limits PstLM ≤ 1 and SVM ≤ 0.4 since 1 Sept 2024.

### Cited Findings
- Behavioural side-effect: under an artificial skylight participants made "riskier as well as more selfish decisions" than under fluorescent light; authors warn daylight-substituting technologies "may profoundly influence mood states and alter decision-making" (n=100) — [Canazei 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S036013231730344X)
- Over-lighting: "over lighting at eye level may hinder arousal, cognitive performances and mood restoration" (artificial window at eye level in a confined office) — [Building and Environment 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132324008588)
- Screen-based windows risk "digital fatigue" from flicker/blue light of high-intensity screens (industry article, weak source) — [AVIXA](https://www.avixa.org/explore/articles/digital-windows-mental-health)
- Parallax illusions work for one viewer only; absence of head-motion parallax makes scenes "less believable" — [Stanford project](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs231a/prev_projects_2016/final-project.pdf); [J Perceptual Imaging](https://library.imaging.org/jpi/articles/3/2/jpi0129)
- Loop repetition: Atmoph views are 15-minute loops — [Atmoph](https://atmoph.com/en/products/aw102); Sky Factory eScape runs unedited 30-minute-to-hours sequences to avoid visible loops — [Sky Factory Japan](https://www.japan.skyfactory.com/eNews/archive/2010/june/); LiquidView films 24-hour views so the scene tracks real time — [TechNewsWorld](https://www.technewsworld.com/story/how-to-add-a-stunning-view-to-a-home-office-or-apartment-anywhere-177732.html)
- Early CoeLux units could only switch on/off with no dimming (2015); current HT25 is DALI-dimmable — [CE Pro](https://www.cepro.com/news/coelux_the_40000_fake_skylight_everyone_will_want/); [Baunetz Wissen](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/himmelsgleiches-deckenpaneel-6557545)
- Night/warm modes: Innerscene A7 sun goes down to 2,500 K and draws as little as 8 W — [Innerscene comparison](https://innerscene.com/a7/competitor-comparison); Yeelight P21 goes to 1,600 K — [homescreen.pl](https://homescreen.pl/en/lighting/69786-yeelight-pro-skylight-p21-intelligent-lighting-6924922245230.html); Misola has scheduled dawn/sunset/night-sky scenes — [Mitsubishi misola](https://www.mitsubishielectric.co.jp/ldg/ja/lighting/products/fixture/misola/introduction.html)
- Heat/power: CoeLux HT25 ~200 W; Innerscene A7 8–200 W; Circadian Sky 35–75 W; Yeelight P21 80 W; Chinese panels 72–120 W; LiquidView three 75" displays (sources in KQ1)
- Melatonin: daytime skylight exposure increased night-time melatonin (n=10); the study title refers to nocturnal light suppressing melatonin, underscoring that the same bright blue-rich panel at night would suppress it — [Yasukouchi 2019](https://jphysiolanthropol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40101-019-0203-4)
- EU Ecodesign (Regulation (EU) 2019/2020) for mains LED light sources: in force 1 Sept 2021 with PstLM ≤ 1 and SVM ≤ 0.9, tightened to SVM ≤ 0.4 from 1 Sept 2024 — [IEA 4E EPREL analysis](https://www.iea-4e.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/03_Market-Trends-over-the-last-decade-EPREL-and-product-test-data.pdf); [Volta ecodesign guide (NL)](https://volta-org.be/media/uhiftokd/nieuwe-richtlijnen-ecodesign-2023.pdf); flicker is measured 0–80 Hz and stroboscopic effect 80 Hz–2 kHz, and luminaires with a separate driver feeding LED modules are not covered by the light-source rule — [Fagerhult](https://fagerhult.ae/de/wissenscenter/technology/driver/flicker-and-tla); no internationally agreed measurement standard for TLM existed as of April 2024 — [MetTLM webinar](https://lightingeurope.org/images/MetTLM_webinar_Dekker.pdf)
- Photobiological safety: RG0 and RG1 under IEC/EN 62471 "are considered non-hazardous and do not require any special measures" — [ERCO](https://www.erco.com/en/designing-with-light/lighting-knowledge/led/blue-light-hazard-7735/); RG2 or higher must be marked on the luminaire and in the manual — [Delta Light](https://deltalight.com/downloads/Photobiological_safety.pdf); EN 60598-1 governs luminaire approval and labelling, and luminaires should be at most RG1 in appropriate use; the light source's risk group can generally be applied to the luminaire — [Trilux](https://www.trilux.com/en/lighting-practice/lighting-equipment/luminaires/photo-biological-safety/)
- Sky Factory lists CE, ANSI/UL and CAN/CSA certification for its light boxes — [Sky Factory guide spec](https://skyfactory.com/guidespec_tsf_lsc_071019)

### Inferences
- For a sleeping cabin the "night sky" scene of Rayleigh products is a trap: it is still short-wavelength scattered light. Specify true off (or ≤ a few lux of warm ≤2,500 K) during the sleep window and reserve blue-rich sky for a scheduled morning dose and the daytime.
- Light leakage: an edge-lit light box or LCD glows uniformly even at low dim and LCD backlights have a floor brightness; a Rayleigh/tunable LED unit on a DALI/0–10 V driver can reach 0.1–1% and hard-off. For sleep, prefer drivers with constant-current reduction or >3 kHz PWM to avoid low-frequency modulation at the dim levels used at night (inference from the Ecodesign TLM limits above).
- The uncanny effect is least likely with a ceiling skylight (people rarely scrutinise ceilings) and most likely with a wall "window" that lacks parallax and shows looping or stock footage; static photographic sky boxes sit in between and are the cheapest to make convincing.
- Chinese OEM panels sold under €500 will need an EU importer declaration (CE under LVD 2014/35/EU and EMC 2014/30/EU), RoHS, an EN 62471 test report and, if sold as a light source, EPREL registration; none of the Alibaba listings cited above mention EN 62471 or SVM test data.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of flicker (PstLM/SVM) or EN 62471 risk group for any of the named products was found.
- No study of light leakage/sleep disruption from a virtual window in a bedroom was found.
- No peer-reviewed treatment of an "uncanny valley" for fake windows exists in the results; evidence is indirect (parallax, loop length).

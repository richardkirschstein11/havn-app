# Lighting for sleep, circadian health and recovery in a windowless sleep cabin (circadian lighting, dawn simulation, evening/night lighting, red/NIR photobiomodulation, components, EU regulation)

Research context: 2.0 x 2.4 m, 2.2 m high windowless cabin (~10.6 m3 air volume), self-service, 24/7, naps of 20 min to overnight stays of 8–12 h, inside commercial buildings in Europe. No staff present; automatic reset between guests.

Method note for the report writer: web search was the only working channel in this session (direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, and the shared web-search budget was exhausted before all planned queries ran). Findings below therefore rest on search-result extracts of the cited pages, not on full-text reads. Where I could not verify a number against the primary paper I say so. Several planned queries (Phillips 2019 PNAS melatonin dose-response; Johner Institute on Annex XVI "high-intensity" scope; Sunricher/Tuya/Casambi controller pricing; Philips/Lumie/Hatch product lux specs; panel heat/fan noise specs; whole-body PBM systematic reviews) could not be run and are listed under Gaps.

## Key Question 1: What concrete light "recipes" (lux, melanopic EDI, CCT) should a sleep cabin offer for arrival/reading, wind-down, sleep, mid-night wake, dawn wake-up, and a daytime "energize" nap exit?

### Takeaway
The only consensus numbers that exist are the Brown et al. 2022 melanopic-EDI targets (daytime >= 250 lx mEDI at the eye; evening, from ~3 h before bed, <= 10 lx mEDI; sleep environment <= 1 lx mEDI, <= 10 lx mEDI only when night-time vision tasks are unavoidable) plus WELL v2 L03 daytime thresholds (150 EML / ~136 mEDI for 1 point, 275 EML / ~250 mEDI for 3 points, measured vertically at eye level). A cabin recipe can be derived by converting these mEDI targets to photopic lux via the melanopic daylight efficacy ratio (MDER) of the chosen LED (roughly 0.45–0.48 at 2700 K, 0.71–0.75 at 4000 K, 0.86–0.97 at 6500 K), using a 30-min 0 -> 100–300 lx dawn ramp for wake-up, and saturated red/amber light for night navigation and sleep-inertia mitigation.

### Cited Findings

**Consensus targets (Brown et al. 2022, PLOS Biology)**
- The paper is Brown et al., PLOS Biology, 17 March 2022, vol. 20(3) e3001571, with 18 co-authors including Brainard, Lockley, Czeisler and Wright; recommendations are expert-consensus based and expressed in melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance (mEDI) as defined in CIE S 026 — [PubMed 35298459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35298459/); [PMC8929548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8929548/)
- Daytime: "the recommended minimum melanopic EDI is 250 lx, using daylight if available or, where addition of electric light is required, via white light with a high melanopic daylight (D65) efficacy ratio" — [CET summary of the consensus](https://cet.org/consensus-recommendations-for-optimum-indoor-lighting/); [CIE TN 015:2023](https://files.cie.co.at/CIE_TN_015_2023.pdf)
- Evening (starting ~3 h before bedtime): recommended maximum melanopic EDI of 10 lx — [CET summary](https://cet.org/consensus-recommendations-for-optimum-indoor-lighting/); [TU Darmstadt paper on the recommendations (10.1177/14771535221120350)](https://tuprints.ulb.tu-darmstadt.de/22956/1/10.1177_14771535221120350.pdf)
- Sleep environment: "should be as dark as possible with a recommended maximum melanopic EDI of 1 lx and 10 lx in case unavoidable activities during the nighttime require vision" — [CET summary](https://cet.org/consensus-recommendations-for-optimum-indoor-lighting/); [CIBSE SLL "Light and sleep" slides](https://www.cibse.org/media/tbabra14/light-and-sleep-sll-event-26th-feb-peter-roger-final.pdf)
- Scope: the recommendations apply to healthy adults (18–55 years) with a day-active schedule and are not meant to override visual, comfort or energy guidance — [CET summary](https://cet.org/consensus-recommendations-for-optimum-indoor-lighting/); [Innerscene summary](https://www.innerscene.com/papers/recommendations-for-healthy-daytime-evening-and-night-time-indoor-light-exposure)
- One popular-press summary (Inverse) states the sleep-room maximum as 10 lux alongside the 1 lux target, which conflicts with the consensus wording; rely on the consensus text — [Inverse](https://inverse.com/mind-body/circadian-rhythm-blue-light-sleep-new-guidelines)

**WELL v2 Feature L03 (Circadian Lighting Design)**
- L03 is worth up to 3 points; thresholds are commonly cited as >= 150 EML (~136 mEDI) for Tier 1/1 point and >= 275 EML (~250 mEDI) for Tier 2/3 points, applied at all (or, per one source, 75% of) workstations for at least 4 h/day starting no later than 12:00 — [Innerscene WELL standards page](https://innerscene.com/well-standards); [Innerscene Circadian Lab](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/circadian-lab); [UPRtek WELL blog](https://www.uprtek.com/en/blogs/how-well-certification-enhances-building-environments-for-better-health-and-well-being)
- Conflicting Tier 2 figure: a WELL AP study guide and a DOE presentation give 240 EML (218 mEDI) for 3 points rather than 275 EML — [open-exam-prep WELL AP guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/well-ap/light-concept/circadian-lighting-design); [DOE SSL R&D 2022 Safranek slides](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2022-02/ssl-rd22_safranek_integration.pdf); contradicted by [Innerscene](https://innerscene.com/well-standards) (275 EML)
- Measurement plane: mEDI is measured on the vertical plane at eye level, not the horizontal work surface; one source gives 1.2 m seated eye height, the study guide gives 18 in (45 cm) above the work plane — [Innerscene Circadian Lab](https://www.innerscene.com/tools/circadian-lab); [open-exam-prep guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/well-ap/light-concept/circadian-lighting-design)
- Dwelling units: levels are achieved in living rooms and kitchens at 55 in (140 cm) in the centre of the room, must be dimmable, and if automated must dim automatically after 8:00 pm; bedrooms/sleeping areas are not addressed in any result found — [open-exam-prep guide](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/well-ap/light-concept/circadian-lighting-design)
- EML = illuminance (lux) x melanopic ratio of the source spectrum; melanopic EDI is tied to CIE S 026:2018 — [UPRtek](https://www.uprtek.com/en/blogs/how-well-certification-enhances-building-environments-for-better-health-and-well-being); [IALD/LIRC WELL guideline 2019 PDF](https://bioslighting.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/2019_IALD-LIRC_WELL_Guidelines-1.pdf)
- A vendor page mentions a newer ANSI/IES RP-46-25 with a >= 250 mEDI daytime minimum; not independently confirmed in this session — [Innerscene](https://innerscene.com/well-standards)

**Converting mEDI to photopic lux: melanopic daylight efficacy ratio (MDER) of LEDs**
- Zumtobel (CRI > 90, CIE S 026/E:2018 basis): MDER ~0.48 at 2700 K, ~0.71 at 4000 K, ~0.95 at 6500 K; MDER is defined in CIE S 026/E:2018 as a conversion factor relative to D65 — [Zumtobel LFE PDF](https://www.zumtobel.com/media/images/42186783_LFE_EN.pdf)
- Lumileds (D65-normalised m/p per CIE S 026): 2700 K = 0.43 (CRI 80) / 0.46 (CRI 90); 6500 K = 0.86 (CRI 80) / 0.97 (CRI 90) — [DOE SSL R&D 2020 Lumileds slides](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2020/02/f71/ssl-rd2020-soer-efficacy_0.pdf)
- XAL (Ra >= 80): melanopic ratio 0.45 for 2700 K, 0.75 for 4000 K — [XAL know-how page](https://www.xal.com/en/know-how/effect-and-correction-factors/)
- A survey of 279 measured 4000 K commercial LED spectra found m-DER between 0.419 and 0.722 (SD 0.051); CCT explained only ~16% of within-class variance, i.e. CCT alone is a poor predictor of melanopic content — reported in search results attributed to either the [CLTC/IES LD+A 2019 article](https://cltc.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk12206/files/media/documents/IES%20LD%2BA%20May%202019%20Research%20-%20Circadian%20Lighting%20Design%20-%20Leveraging%20the%20Melanopic%20Efficacy%20of%20Luminou.pdf) or [photonics.pl letter](https://photonics.pl/PLP/index.php/letters/article/view/18-8) (attribution not verified)

**Evidence for specific scene elements**
- Dawn ramp protocols used in research: light starts below 1 lux and rises linearly to its peak over 30 min, with peaks of 100, 200 or 300 lx — [PMC270037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC270037); a 2020 study used 0 -> 210 lx polychromatic light over the 30 min before scheduled wake — [ECNU J. Psychological Science 2020](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2020/V43/I3/526); van de Werken et al. 2010 used 30 min artificial dawn with max 300 lx — [Gabel et al. 2013 PDF](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/171514/1/Gabel_CI_2013.pdf)
- Morning light after sleep restriction (Gabel 2013, n = 17, 48-h lab protocol, two 6-h nights): performance decreased after dim light (< 8 lx), stayed stable after blue light (100 lx, 470 nm) and improved after dawn-simulation light; dawn simulation improved subjective well-being, mood and cognitive performance "with minimal impact on circadian phase"; blue light advanced melatonin onset — [Gabel et al. 2013, Chronobiol Int 30(8):988–997](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/171514/1/Gabel_CI_2013.pdf); [Innerscene summary](https://www.innerscene.com/papers/effects-of-artificial-dawn-and-morning-blue-light-on-daytime-cognitive-performance-well-being-cortisol-and-melatonin-levels)
- Saturated red light for sleep inertia (Figueiro et al. 2019, n = 30, crossover): red light mask during sleep and red goggles on waking improved some task scores vs dim light; authors conclude "saturated red light delivered through closed eyelids at levels that do not suppress melatonin can be used to mitigate sleep inertia upon waking" — [PubMed 31118850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31118850/); [Dove Press full text](https://www.dovepress.com/effects-of-red-light-on-sleep-inertia-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NSS)
- Figueiro on red light: "I would not make the claim that (red light) promotes sleep"; its benefit is minimising circadian disruption when used in the evening — [KSL interview](https://www.ksl.com/article/50657640/how-red-light-can-affect-your-sleep)
- Red light before bed can backfire: Pan et al. 2023 report that red light "can increase subjective alertness, anxiety, and negative emotions in both healthy subjects and people with insomnia disorder, which can affect sleep directly or indirectly" — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1200350/full); [PMC10484593](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10484593/)
- Night lighting for safe movement: LHRC (Mount Sinai) reports visual-cue night lighting (horizontal/vertical light lines) reduced night-time falls among older adults in institutional settings by more than 30%; the 2012 Age & Ageing trial (adults >= 65) found better postural control under the cue-lighting conditions than under conventional plug-in night lights — [Buildings.com on LHRC](https://www.buildings.com/resiliency-sustainability/health-wellness-iaq/article/55259045/lhrc-study-finds-leds-help-prevent-falls-among-older-adults); [Figueiro et al. 2012 PDF](https://healthdesign.org/sites/default/files/article-pdfs/Figueiro_Plitnik_Rea_Gras_Rea-2012CHD-KPS.pdf)
- Flicker: EU Ecodesign Regulation 2019/2020 (in force 1 Sept 2021) limits PstLM <= 1.0 (visible flicker, 0–80 Hz) and SVM <= 0.4 (stroboscopic effect, 80 Hz–2 kHz); limits formally apply to mains-powered light sources, while luminaires with a separate driver are excluded — [Fagerhult flicker & TLA](https://www.fagerhult.com/knowledge/technology/driver/flicker-and-tla/); [ERCO flicker](https://erco.com/en/designing-with-light/lighting-knowledge/led/flicker-7734); [eceee on SVM 0.4 evidence](https://www.eceee.org/all-news/news/new-study-provides-evidence-for-more-stringent-flicker-requirements-in-pending-ecodesign-lighting-regulations)

### Inferences
- Derived cabin recipe (all values vertical at the eye of a lying/sitting guest; photopic lux computed as mEDI / MDER using the Zumtobel/Lumileds ratios above):

| Scene | Target mEDI at eye | Suggested CCT | Photopic lux needed at eye | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrival / reading (daytime stays) | 250 (Brown daytime min; WELL Tier 2 ~250) | 4000–5000 K, CRI >= 90 | ~350 lx at 4000 K (MDER ~0.71); ~265 lx at 6500 K (0.95) | Brown's daytime floor is for day-active people; it is also the obvious "energize" level |
| Arrival / reading (evening, < 3 h before intended sleep) | <= 10 | 2200–2700 K | <= ~21 lx at 2700 K (0.48); allow up to ~30–50 lx with amber ~2000 K (MDER assumed ~0.2–0.3, not sourced) | Reading at <= 10 mEDI is feasible only with a warm, localised reading light close to the page; keep ambient lower |
| Wind-down (last 20–30 min) | 1–5 | amber/red, < 2200 K | a few lux | Avoid saturated red at high intensity: Pan 2023 links red light to higher alertness/anxiety |
| Sleep | <= 1 (ideally ~0) | n/a | < ~2 lx at 2700 K; red indicator/night light at ~0.5–1 lx photopic | Windowless cabin can reach true darkness; keep status LEDs off or deep red |
| Mid-night wake / bathroom navigation | <= 10 (Brown: 10 lx only when vision tasks unavoidable) | deep red or amber | 5–20 lx photopic of long-wavelength light ~ well under 10 mEDI (red has near-zero melanopic content, inference) | Use low floor-level cue lines (LHRC visual-cue approach) rather than overhead light |
| Dawn wake-up (overnight stays) | ramp to ~100–250 | 2000 K -> 4000–5000 K over 30 min | 0 -> 100–300 lx over 30 min (research protocols) | Matches van de Werken/Gabel/ECNU protocols; stays within Brown daytime targets at the end of the ramp |
| Nap exit "energize" (daytime) | >= 250 for 5–15 min | 5000–6500 K | ~265–350 lx | Supported by Brown daytime floor and Gabel 2013 (dawn-sim and 100 lx blue preserved performance after sleep restriction); Figueiro 2019 suggests red through closed eyelids as a melatonin-neutral alternative |
| Nap exit (evening/night, e.g. shift workers) | keep <= 10 if the guest intends to sleep again within 3 h; otherwise a short burst of brighter light is a trade-off | 2700 K or red | <= ~21 lx at 2700 K | Brown recommendations explicitly exclude shift-work schedules; no consensus guidance exists for this case |

- Because MDER varies by ~0.42–0.72 even among 4000 K LEDs, the cabin spec should require the LED supplier to provide a measured SPD and a computed mEDI (CIE S 026 toolbox) for each channel at max output, rather than specifying CCT alone.
- A tunable-white fixture whose warm channel is ~2200–2700 K cannot get below ~0.45 MDER; to reach <= 1 mEDI at usable night-navigation brightness the cabin needs a separate amber/red channel (RGBW or dedicated red strip), not just tunable white.
- Brown's 10-lx evening ceiling means "reading on arrival" for an evening/overnight guest should be a localised, warm reading light (bedside, aimed at the page), while the ambient scene stays low.

### Gaps
- Phillips et al. 2019 (PNAS) dose-response data on evening melatonin suppression (half-maximal suppression at a median of roughly 24 lx with very large inter-individual variability) is in my background knowledge but could not be searched/verified in this session; treat as unverified.
- No source found giving the MDER of amber (~1800–2200 K) or deep-red LED channels; the 0.2–0.3 figure in the table is an assumption and must be replaced by measured SPD data.
- The official IWBI text of WELL v2 L03 could not be fetched; the 275 vs 240 EML discrepancy (likely version differences) and the 75% vs 100% workstation coverage discrepancy remain unresolved.
- No study was found on lighting recipes for short (20-min) daytime naps specifically; recommendations above are extrapolated from sleep-inertia and consensus literature.
- No study was found addressing light exposure guidance for nap/sleep cabins used by shift workers at night; Brown et al. explicitly scope out shift schedules.

## Key Question 2: How strong is the evidence for dawn simulation on sleep inertia and mood?

### Takeaway
Dawn simulation has consistent but small-sample evidence (n = 8 to ~21 per lab study; one n = 40 clinical SAD study) that it reduces subjective sleep-inertia complaints and improves subjective alertness, mood and, in one sleep-restriction study, cognitive performance; objective markers (cortisol, core body temperature, most performance tests) are mostly null, and the mechanism is unclear. Reviews describe the countermeasure literature as mixed and methodologically limited; there is no large parallel-group RCT.

### Cited Findings
- Giménez & Hessels 2010: use of an artificial dawn light "resulted in a significant reduction of sleep inertia complaints, which could not be explained by a shift in the dim light melatonin onset or timing of sleep offset" — [Innerscene summary of Giménez et al.](https://www.innerscene.com/papers/effects-of-artificial-dawn-on-subjective-ratings-of-sleep-inertia-and-dim-light-melatonin-onset); a 2024 review notes that an alarm clock with dawn simulation "significantly reduced complaints of sleep inertia, though the mechanism for how this occurred was unclear" — [Sleep-inertia countermeasures review, PMC10969141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10969141)
- van de Werken et al. 2010: 30 min artificial dawn (max 300 lx) before wake-up was more efficient at alleviating sleep inertia and increasing subjective alertness than acute light at wake-up; no significant effects on cognitive performance, core body temperature or cortisol; benefit attributed to accelerated skin-temperature decline after rising and lighter sleep before the alarm — [Gabel et al. 2013 PDF citing van de Werken](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/171514/1/Gabel_CI_2013.pdf); [VU Amsterdam record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/effects-of-artificial-dawn-on-sleep-inertia-skin-temperature-and-/)
- Gabel et al. 2013 (n = 17, aged 20–35, two 6-h sleep-restriction nights): dawn simulation improved subjective well-being, mood and cognitive performance vs dim and blue light; salivary cortisol at wake-up was higher after dawn simulation than after blue or dim light; blue light (100 lx, 470 nm) advanced melatonin onset — [Gabel et al. 2013, Chronobiol Int](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/171514/1/Gabel_CI_2013.pdf); follow-up: effects of morning light depend on individual performance level and cognitive domain — [Gabel et al. 2015, Behav Brain Res 281:258–266](https://www.chronobiology.ch/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/7.pdf)
- Cortisol: Gabel et al. 2013 note that the same Dutch team "did not confirm enhanced cortisol levels upon awakening after dawn," contradicting earlier work — [Gabel 2013 PDF](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/171514/1/Gabel_CI_2013.pdf); Philips' user manual nonetheless states that light increases cortisol production (manufacturer material, not peer-reviewed) — [Philips manual PDF](https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20211006/994c4d382ce94428ac05adb900b370bb.pdf)
- Thompson et al. 2014 (n = 8 people with difficulty waking; randomised order, control vs dawn simulation starting 30 min before waking; Eur J Appl Physiol 114(5):1049–1056): dawn simulation accelerated the post-waking decline in skin temperature and distal–proximal gradient, "coincident with, but not necessarily causally related to" sleep-inertia dissipation; no other effects on performance, core body temperature or cortisol — [LJMU repository PDF](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/6186/1/590088.pdf); as summarised in [Figueiro et al. 2019 (Dove Press)](https://www.dovepress.com/effects-of-red-light-on-sleep-inertia-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NSS)
- 2020 Chinese study (n = 21 students, within-subject, dawn 0 -> 210 lx over 30 min vs constant light vs no light): dawn simulation improved subjective alertness and some performance measures; no differential effect on negative mood or the 2-back task; benefit attributed to the dynamic light pattern — [ECNU J. Psychological Science 2020, 43(3):526](https://jps.ecnu.edu.cn/EN/Y2020/V43/I3/526)
- Review verdict: outcomes of environmental countermeasure studies "are mixed due to methodological limitations such as the lack of a control group" — [PMC10969141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10969141)
- Mood/SAD: a one-week study of 40 winter depressives compared a Lumie light box (4,300 lx) with a Lumie Bodyclock dawn simulator (ramp over the last 30 min of sleep peaking at 100 lx at 50 cm); depression scores fell by similar amounts and preference split almost evenly — reported in a Lumie press release, so treat framing with caution — [Cambridge Network / Lumie](https://www.cambridgenetwork.co.uk/news/dawn-simulators-are-useful-tool-treatment-sad-reports-lumie); [Cision](https://news.cision.com/lumie/r/dawn-simulators-are-a-useful-tool-in-the-treatment-of-sad-reports-lumie,c9764574)
- Dawn simulation has been studied clinically since David Avery's 1980s trials; its origin is an 1890 patent — [Wikipedia: Dawn simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_simulation)
- Lumie's product launch claims that wake-up lights "improve sleep quality and awakening, along with mood, energy and productivity" without citing specific studies — [Cambridge Network, Bodyclock Luxe launch](https://www.cambridgenetwork.co.uk/news/lumie-launches-new-premium-bodyclock-luxe-optimal-sleep-and-waking)
- Red light as an alternative sleep-inertia countermeasure (Figueiro 2019, n = 30 crossover, three Friday nights): sleepiness fell and performance improved in all conditions over 30 min; some tasks were significantly better in the red-mask condition than dim light; goggle scores improved after a few minutes of wear — [PubMed 31118850](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31118850/); [PMC6506010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6506010)

### Inferences
- The effect that replicates across studies is subjective (fewer sleep-inertia complaints, higher alertness/mood); objective gains are inconsistent and small. For a cabin, dawn simulation is a low-risk, user-valued feature whose marketing should say "wake more gently / less groggy," not "proven to improve performance or cortisol."
- The research protocols converge on a 30-min linear ramp to 100–300 lx at the pillow; higher final brightness has no demonstrated extra benefit in these studies, and a 250–300 lx, 4000–5000 K end-point aligns with Brown's daytime 250 mEDI floor.
- Because van de Werken 2010 found the gradual dawn beat acute light at wake-up, the cabin should ramp before the alarm rather than switch on bright light at alarm time; for 20-min naps the ramp must be compressed (e.g. 5–10 min), for which there is no direct evidence.

### Gaps
- Sample size and setting of Giménez & Hessels 2010 could not be verified from search extracts (my background recollection is a home study of about 100 participants using a Philips Wake-up Light; unverified).
- No peer-reviewed trial of the Philips SmartSleep/HF3650, Hatch Restore or Casper Glow products was found; product lux specs could not be searched (budget exhausted).
- No parallel-group RCT with n > 50 on dawn simulation and sleep inertia was identified.

## Key Question 3: What is the state of evidence for red/NIR light therapy on sleep quality, melatonin, recovery and "longevity" markers? Which claims are supported, which are marketing?

### Takeaway
Supported with moderate (but low-quality) evidence: pre-exercise/post-exercise PBM improves muscular endurance and reduces fatigue/soreness markers (39 RCTs, n = 861, "very low to moderate" quality). Weak/mixed: sleep (one n = 20 non-randomised 2012 cohort behind most "melatonin" claims; a 2022 double-blind RCT of 850 nm found no sleep or circadian effect; phototherapy meta-analysis shows short-term, heterogeneous benefit mainly in older adults); cognition (small within-subject 1064 nm studies); skin (older split-face trials, n = 76 and n = 136). Not supported by evidence found: "longevity," systemic anti-ageing, mitochondrial-health or inflammation claims in healthy adults, beyond a single n = 20 retinal-function study.

### Cited Findings

**Sleep and melatonin**
- Zhao et al. 2012 (J Athl Train 47(6):673): 20 Chinese female basketball players split into red-light (n = 10, 30 min whole-body red light nightly for 14 days) and placebo (n = 10, no light); design labelled a cohort study; reported improved PSQI, serum melatonin and 12-min run, with sleep-score change correlating with melatonin change (r = -0.695, P = .006) — [PMC3499892](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499892); [J Athl Train record](https://nata.kglmeridian.com/view/journals/attr/47/6/article-p673.xml); a vendor blog quotes a "75% melatonin increase" from it that could not be verified and some sites wrongly call it an RCT — [Mito Red blog](https://mitoredlight.com/blogs/mito-red-blog/red-light-therapy-sleep-energy-and-performance)
- Giménez et al. 2022/2023 (Biology; double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, 850 nm NIR in healthy people with mild sleep complaints): only the 6.5 J/cm2 dose showed consistent benefits for well-being (better mood, less drowsiness, lower resting heart rate); "no significant effects on sleep or circadian rhythms" — [PubMed 36671752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36671752/); [PMC9855677](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9855677/)
- Pan et al. 2023: red light increased subjective alertness, anxiety and negative emotions in healthy and insomnia subjects, potentially worsening sleep — [Frontiers in Psychiatry 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1200350/full)
- 2024 meta-analysis of phototherapy (light-based interventions broadly, 12 trials): significant short-term improvement in sleep quality vs placebo, not significant 2–4 weeks after cessation; I2 ~ 91–94%; benefit confined to older adults (MD = -2.70, 95% CI -3.59 to -2.29), none in adolescents/middle-aged — reported in search extracts as Chen & Wu 2024; most likely [PMC11274037](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11274037) (attribution per search summary, not verified)
- Systematic review of PBMT in older adults (Biomedicines 2024; 406 records screened, 10 included): mixed results, highly variable protocols, not sleep-focused — [MDPI Biomedicines 12(7):1409](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9059/12/7/1409)
- 2025 systematic review on transcranial PBM for sleep, wakefulness and cognition: argues t-PBM "may improve sleep quality"; narrative, no pooled estimate — [Frontiers Behav Neurosci 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2025.1542462/pdf); [PMC12350269](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12350269/)
- A vendor blog cites a "2026 meta-analysis of five sham-controlled trials (240 participants)" with PSQI improvement; original could not be located — [Lumaflex blog](https://www.lumaflex.com/blogs/lumaflex-news/home-photobiomodulation-sleep-disorders) (unverified)
- Narrative review (Jung & Kim 2024, Sleep Medicine Research) describes PBM as red/NIR wavelengths triggering photochemical reactions without significant heat — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/32506441f94f4fefaeb3e3f7c19545c8)
- The mechanistic rationale for red light at night is that long wavelengths have minimal impact on circadian photoreceptors and do not suppress melatonin — a plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated sleep outcome — [Mito Red "science of red light and sleep"](https://mitoredlight.com/blogs/mito-red-blog/the-science-of-red-light-and-sleep) (vendor)

**Muscle recovery / performance**
- Vanin et al. systematic review & meta-analysis (searches to March 2017; 39 RCTs, 861 participants, 28 pooled vs placebo): PBM improved time to exhaustion, number of repetitions, isometric peak torque and blood lactate, with "very low to moderate quality evidence"; authors call for better-quality trials — [Amsterdam UMC record](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/publications/photobiomodulation-therapy-for-the-improvement-of-muscular-perfor/); [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/41783); [PMC6546960](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6546960/)
- Leal-Junior et al. 2015 (Lasers Med Sci 30(2):925–939): systematic review with meta-analysis of laser/LED phototherapy on exercise performance and recovery markers — [UNESP repository](https://repositorio.unesp.br/entities/publication/8cf3763f-ee7e-43e2-aa12-2a4eac38a609); vendor pages cite a "2016 Leal-Junior meta-analysis, 22 RCTs, n = 634" with reduced CK and DOMS, details not matching verified records — [Mito Red evidence page](https://mitoredlight.com/pages/red-light-therapy-for-muscle-recovery-performance-clinical-evidence) (vendor, unreliable)
- A 2018 clinical-recommendations paper (Braz J Phys Ther) states the evidence supports a dose "therapeutic window" for performance and recovery and calls for CONSORT/TIDieR reporting — [PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/41783)
- A newsletter summarises a recent 13-RCT meta-analysis of PBMT before/after exercise vs sham on soreness, pressure-pain threshold and peak torque; year and total n not given — [FoundMyFitness Science Digest](https://www.foundmyfitness.com/stories/fuvl3c)

**Skin**
- Only pre-2018 primary trials were found: a 2007 split-face, placebo-controlled LED study (n = 76) reported wrinkle reduction up to 36%, elasticity increase up to 19% and biopsy-confirmed collagen/elastin increases — [PubMed 17566756](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17566756/) (old, pre-2018); vendor pages cite a 2014 RCT (n = 136, photoaging, 30 sessions, polychromatic red/NIR vs sham) with improved periorbital wrinkles, roughness and complexion — [Mito Red skin evidence page](https://mitoredlight.com/pages/red-light-therapy-for-skin-and-anti-aging-clinical-evidence); [Lumaflex trial summary](https://www.lumaflex.com/blogs/clinical-trials/light-therapy-for-anti-aging-a-study-on-red-near-infrared-efficacy-patient-satisfaction-and-skin-texture-light-therapy-for-anti-aging) (both vendor; year/results unverified)
- A review wiki on LLLT skin rejuvenation frames benefits as "supposed," i.e. hypothesis-level — [Forever Healthy wiki](https://forever-healthy.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ENG/pages/2559112/Skin+Rejuvenation+by+Low-Level+Light+Therapy)

**Cognition**
- Sham-controlled within-subject study of 61 healthy older adults: 7 days x 12 min of 1064 nm laser at 250 mW/cm2 over the left DLPFC improved N-back working memory, with 3-week follow-up — [PMC9514540](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9514540/); [Neurophotonics 9(3):035005](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/neurophotonics/volume-9/issue-3/035005/Repeated-transcranial-photobiomodulation-improves-working-memory-of-healthy-older-adults/10.1117/1.NPh.9.3.035005.pdf)
- Single-session study of 90 young adults: ~25% working-memory score improvement after 1064 nm over the right PFC; no effect at 852 nm or over the left PFC — [Optica OPN news](https://www.optica-opn.org/home/newsroom/2022/december/can_low-power_light_improve_working_memory/) (news report, design unverified)
- 2022 systematic review of 35 tPBM studies: 29 (82.9%) reported positive cognitive effects, but only about half were RCTs — [PubMed 36371017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36371017/)
- 2025 randomised single-blind sham-controlled crossover (n = 55 healthy older adults, 1064 nm): working-memory and resting-state network changes — [NJIT record](https://researchwith.njit.edu/en/publications/transcranial-photobiomodulation-improves-functional-brain-network/)

**Mitochondria / "longevity"**
- UCL study (Sci Rep 2021, n = 20): a single 3-min exposure to 670 nm deep red light between 8–9 am improved colour-contrast vision by ~17% on average, lasting at least a week; the same exposure in the afternoon had no effect; mechanism (reduced interfacial water viscosity around ATP synthase) is a hypothesis — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/935701); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/11/211124154118.htm); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/newsroom/can-red-light-exposure-improve-eyesight)
- The same press coverage notes the small sample and that an earlier 2-week daily protocol is a different design — [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/newsroom/can-red-light-exposure-improve-eyesight)

### Inferences
- The strongest commercially usable claim for a cabin "Health Pod" is exercise recovery/performance support (meta-analytic evidence, albeit low quality), followed by "relaxation / mood" (Giménez 2022 NIR found mood/drowsiness/heart-rate benefits without sleep effects). "Improves sleep" rests on one 2012 n = 20 non-randomised study and heterogeneous phototherapy meta-analyses, and "boosts melatonin" rests on that same study; both should be avoided or hedged ("may support").
- "Longevity," "anti-ageing," "mitochondrial health," "reduces inflammation" for healthy adults are marketing extrapolations from mechanism papers and small studies; no human trial with longevity or systemic inflammation endpoints in healthy adults surfaced.
- Timing matters in the opposite direction from vendor advice: the UCL 670 nm effect was morning-only, and Pan 2023 found red light raises alertness/anxiety. A pre-sleep red-light session in the cabin is therefore not supported; a daytime or post-exercise session is the defensible use.

### Gaps
- The Li et al. 2024 (Sleep Medicine) meta-analysis on red/NIR light and sleep in healthy subjects, and Lopes et al. 2023 (Lasers Med Sci) on PBM and sleep in older adults, were named in search extracts but could not be located or read; their effect sizes are unknown.
- No 2018+ RCT on whole-body panel PBM for sleep in healthy adults was found; the Giménez 2022 study used localised 850 nm exposure, not a panel.
- No human study on whole-body PBM and inflammatory or ageing biomarkers in healthy adults was found (search budget exhausted before the planned query ran).
- The 2022 Sports Medicine meta-analysis on mode-specific exercise performance (search to May 2022) was mentioned but its pooled results could not be retrieved.

## Key Question 4: What irradiance and session protocol is standard for consumer/pro panels, and what panel size/power/heat is realistic inside a 2.0 x 2.4 m cabin?

### Takeaway
Consumer/pro panels advertise roughly 85–160 mW/cm2 at 6 in (15 cm), typically measured at the panel centre; half-body panels (~36 x 12 in) draw ~445–490 W and weigh ~15 kg, which is manageable in the cabin, but 450 W of waste heat in a sealed 10.6 m3 room is a ventilation problem that must be designed for. Session protocols in the literature cluster around 10–20 min at 15–30 cm, with the only well-characterised sleep-adjacent dose (Giménez 2022) being 6.5 J/cm2.

- Chinese "1000W"-class panel (Idealight IL-TL1500): 900 x 3 W chips, 650 mA drive, 50,000 h life, listed "actual power" 1800 W, wavelengths customisable 380–1050 nm — [Beautetrade listing](https://www.beautetrade.com/products/shenzhen-factory-wholesale1000w-2000w-led-panel-lights-850nm-660nm-infra-red-therapy-light-for-home/24406/); wattage labels on these listings are inconsistent ("1000W" vs 1800 W actual) — [Beautetrade TL1500 listing](https://beautetrade.com/products/china-supplier-red-light-therapy-idealight-red-infrared-light-therapy-panels-850nm-660nm-tl1500-for-full-body/24377)
- Measurement caveats: consumer solar-meter (broadband) readings differ from calibrated spectroradiometer readings; irradiance is quoted at a stated distance (usually 6 in) and manufacturer reports often cover only the panel centre — [Dr. Axe](https://draxe.com/healthy-home/best-red-light-therapy-panels/); [Stacking Years](https://www.stackingyears.com/blog/best-red-light-therapy-device-2026)
- Dose actually tested for well-being: 6.5 J/cm2 of 850 nm (the only dose with consistent benefit in the double-blind RCT) — [PubMed 36671752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36671752/)
- Transcranial protocol example: 12 min/day at 250 mW/cm2, 1064 nm, for 7 days — [PMC9514540](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9514540/)
- Retinal/mitochondrial protocol example: 3 min of 670 nm, morning — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/935701)
- Vendor sleep protocol example: 30 min whole-body red light nightly for 14 days (Zhao 2012) — [PMC3499892](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499892)

### Inferences
- Dose arithmetic: J/cm2 = (mW/cm2 x seconds)/1000. At a true (not centre-peak) 50 mW/cm2 at 15–20 cm, 10 min delivers 30 J/cm2 and 20 min 60 J/cm2; at 100 mW/cm2, 10 min = 60 J/cm2. The 6.5 J/cm2 well-being dose from Giménez 2022 would take ~1–2 min at panel irradiances, so a cabin session of 10–15 min is at the high end of what has been tested for well-being and in line with muscle-recovery protocols.
- Geometry: a half-body panel (36 x 12 in ~ 91 x 30 cm) mounted on the 2.4 m long wall beside the bed at 15–30 cm from the body, or a slim full-length strip-style panel above/behind the bed, fits a 2.0 x 2.4 m footprint; a free-standing full-body tower is impractical because the guest cannot stand 15 cm from it with a bed occupying most of the floor.
- Heat: a 450–490 W panel running 15 min dissipates ~400–440 kJ. With 10.6 m3 of air (~12.7 kg, cp ~1.0 kJ/kg·K), that is a ~30 K temperature rise if nothing is removed; even with wall/bed thermal mass absorbing most of it, the cabin needs a ventilation rate on the order of 50–100 m3/h during and after a session (my estimate) and the panel's own fans will add noise. Chinese "1000W" panels listed at 1800 W actual would be unworkable thermally and electrically in this space.
- Because measured irradiance falls off sharply away from the panel centre, specifications to the Guangzhou integrator should require a 9-point irradiance map at the actual mounting distance (e.g. 20 cm), measured with a calibrated spectroradiometer, not a single centre reading.

### Gaps
- No source on panel surface temperature, fan noise (dB) or heat-dissipation figures was retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- No independent lab report for Joovv or PlatinumLED could be read directly; irradiance figures above are second-hand.
- Mito Red MitoPRO 1500 dimensions were not found.
- No evidence-based "standard" session protocol exists for whole-body panels; the 10–20 min, 15–30 cm, 3–5x/week pattern is industry convention, not a guideline from a standards body.

## Key Question 5: What are the regulatory boundaries in the EU for offering red-light sessions commercially (cosmetic/wellness vs. medical device under MDR, photobiological safety classification, warnings)?

### Takeaway
Any medical claim (pain, sleep disorder, wound healing, etc.) makes the panel a medical device under MDR 2017/745; even without a medical claim, MDR Annex XVI section 5 pulls "high-intensity electromagnetic radiation emitting equipment intended for use on the human body ... for skin treatment" into the MDR with Common Specifications (Reg. 2022/2346, applicable from 22 June 2023, transition to June 2028) and class IIb (Reg. 2022/2347), so skin-related claims for a red-light panel are high-risk from a compliance standpoint. Whether a low-irradiance LED wellness panel is "high-intensity" and therefore in scope is not settled in any source found; a pure "relaxation/recovery/well-being" positioning with no skin, medical or physiological-outcome claims is the lowest-risk route, with photobiological safety classified per EN 62471 (risk group labelling required for RG2 and above) and, if sold as an electrical product, the standard LVD/EMC CE route.

### Cited Findings
- Annex XVI section 5 (MDR) covers: "High-intensity electromagnetic radiation (e.g. infra-red, visible light and ultra-violet) emitting equipment intended for use on the human body, including coherent and non-coherent sources, monochromatic and broad spectrum, such as lasers and intense pulsed light equipment, for skin resurfacing, tattoo or hair removal or other skin treatment" — [NAMSA on Annex XVI implementing regulation](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/mdr-implementing-regulation-annex-xvi-published/); [BSI Annex XVI whitepaper](https://bsigroup.com/siteassets/pdf/en/insights-and-media/insights/white-papers/bsi-md-mdr-annex-xvi-whitepaper-en-gb.pdf)
- Common Specifications: Regulation (EU) 2022/2346 applies from 22 June 2023 with a transition period to June 2028 (with exceptions); its annexes set per-product requirements and Annex I covers risk management — [QbD Group](https://www.qbdgroup.com/en/blog/annex-xvi-products-with-non-medical-purpose-compliance-with-mdr-and-common-specifications); [Donawa](https://www.donawa.com/global-news/eu-commission-publishes-mdr-article-xvi-common-specifications/)
- Classification: Regulation 2022/2347 reclassifies Annex XVI section 5 skin-treatment light equipment as class IIb, unless intended for hair removal only (class IIa) — [NAMSA](https://namsa.com/resources/blog/mdr-implementing-regulation-annex-xvi-published/); [Johner Institute blog](https://blog.johner-institute.com/?p=10290)
- Obelis: the MDR introduced an "Aesthetic Devices" category for products "without an intended medical purpose" that claim effects on the body, covering light-therapy devices — [Obelis on red/blue light therapy devices and EU compliance](https://www.obelis.net/news/red-and-blue-light-therapy-devices-and-eu-compliance/)
- An Alibaba seller blog claims therapeutic claims make a device class IIa while wellness-only devices can self-certify as class I; this is a commercial source that conflicts with the Annex XVI/2022/2347 framework and should not be relied on — [Alibaba seller blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/red-light-therapy/ce-certification-stainless-steel-guide-alibaba-b2b)
- Photobiological safety: IEC/EN 62471 applies to any non-laser light source and sorts lamps into RG0–RG3 by permissible exposure time (RG1 ~10,000 s, RG2 ~100 s, RG3 0.25 s); RG2 or higher must be marked on the product and in the manual — [Delta Light photobiological safety PDF](https://deltalight.com/downloads/Photobiological_safety.pdf); [ERCO blue-light hazard](https://erco.com/en/designing-with-light/lighting-knowledge/led/blue-light-hazard-7735); [Radioprotection 2017 table](https://www.radioprotection.org/articles/radiopro/full_html/2017/04/radiopro170025/T1.html)
- IEC 62471 is a performance/classification standard without constructional requirements — [Intertek: photobiological compliance for medical devices](https://w3inte.intertek.com/blog/2024/09-17-photobiological-compliance-for-medical-devices)
- NIR hazard: most IREDs emit 800–960 nm; radiation in this band "causes a thermal retina hazard and thermal injury risk of the cornea and possible delayed effects on the lens"; IEC 62471 has a separate retinal thermal limit for 780–1400 nm sources with a weak visual stimulus (i.e. where the aversion response does not protect) — [Osram/ams "Eye safety of IREDs" PDF](https://www.mikrocontroller.net/attachment/430118/Eye_Safety_of_IREDs_used_in_Lamp_Applications.pdf); [Vishay eye-safety note](https://www.vishay.com:443/docs/81935/eyesafe.pdf); [Renesas AN1737](https://www.renesas.com/eu/en/doc/application-note/an1737.pdf)
- Risk group depends on distance: a lighting-product test report shows RG2 at 20 cm and RG1 at 30 cm for 380–1400 nm thermal retinal radiance — [Hubbell case study PDF](https://hubbellcdn.com/CaseStudies/HHI-CHL-CH-UK-03299.pdf)
- Eye protection: a regulatory Q&A states that if components are exempt under IEC/EN 62471 eye protection is not required by regulation, but may be if operated atypically; manufacturers should use accredited labs for classification — [EHS Daily Advisor](https://ehsdailyadvisor.com/?p=31086)
- Device standards: IEC 60601-2-83 (home light therapy equipment) was cited under the MDR and specifically considers untrained users, children and pets in non-professional settings; the 2026 adoption of EN IEC 60601-2-57 (non-laser light source equipment for medical and aesthetic use) gives a formal presumption of conformity to EU law — [Intertek](https://w3inte.intertek.com/blog/2024/09-17-photobiological-compliance-for-medical-devices); [CEN-CENELEC news on EN IEC 60601-2-57:2026](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2026/en-in-the-spotlight/2026-02-27-en-iec-60601-2-57-2026/); [Mandate M/565](https://iteh.es/catalog/mandate/clc/2f1f463d-32a3-4ab8-9274-0f1a23504e6f/m-565)
- Contrast with the US: LED light-therapy products are cleared by FDA as medical devices via 510(k) for specific indications (e.g. ELIXIR MD K241293; HigherDose Red Light Hat K242363) — [Innolitics K241293](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K241293); [Innolitics K242363](https://fda.innolitics.com/device/K242363)
- Flicker/ecodesign for the general cabin lighting: Regulation 2019/2020 PstLM <= 1.0, SVM <= 0.4 for mains-powered light sources — [Fagerhult](https://www.fagerhult.com/knowledge/technology/driver/flicker-and-tla/)

### Inferences
- Decision tree for the cabin operator: (1) no health/skin/medical claim, positioned as "relaxation/warm light experience" -> consumer electrical product (LVD/EMC/RoHS CE, EN 62471 report, instructions with eye-exposure warnings); (2) skin claims ("collagen", "anti-ageing", "skin rejuvenation") -> likely Annex XVI section 5, class IIb, notified body, Common Specifications 2022/2346 (including, per the CS framework, user information and risk-management requirements); (3) health claims (pain, sleep disorder, wound) -> full MDR medical device. The "high-intensity" qualifier in section 5 is undefined in the sources found, so an OEM LED panel marketed for skin could not be assumed to be out of scope.
- For a self-service cabin with no staff, the practical safety package is: automatic session timer and maximum daily exposure lock per booking, panel mounted so direct on-axis viewing at < 30 cm is unlikely, in-cabin eye-protection (disposable goggles) or an interlock that requires the user to acknowledge eye-protection instructions, RG labelling per the 62471 report, thermal cut-out, and a pictogram warning for photosensitising medication, pregnancy and epilepsy (standard industry warnings; not verified against a specific regulation in this session).
- Commercial provision to the public (vs. sale) does not remove product-law obligations; the cabin manufacturer placing the integrated panel on the EU market is the "manufacturer" under the relevant directives/regulations for the combined product.

### Gaps
- The definition/threshold of "high-intensity" in Annex XVI section 5 and whether typical 660/850 nm LED panels (~100 mW/cm2) fall inside it could not be verified; the Johner Institute scope query did not run (search budget exhausted). This is the single most important regulatory question and needs a notified-body or regulatory-counsel opinion.
- The exact labelling/warning wording required by Common Specifications 2022/2346 Annex for section 5 devices was not retrieved.
- No IEC 62471 classification report for an actual 660/850 nm consumer panel was found; the risk group of such panels at 15–30 cm is unknown from sources (vendor claims of "RG0/RG1" were not located).
- National rules on operating light-therapy equipment commercially (e.g. German NiSV for optical radiation in cosmetic use, which from memory requires operator training for certain high-intensity sources) could not be searched; flag as a gap to verify for Germany specifically.

### Inferences
- Rough budget per cabin (my estimate from the figures above): PBM panel US$300–600 at OEM FOB for a 300–500 W half-body unit in 1–50 volume, plausibly US$200–400 at 500+ units with custom branding (not quoted by any source); or US$1,300–1,700 for a branded BioMax/Mito/Kala Elite-class panel. Circadian lighting: a 4-channel (tunable white + amber/red) 24 V strip system with a DALI-2 DT8 or Casambi driver is a low-hundreds-of-dollars BOM; SunLike/Optisolis-class LEDs add a premium over generic 2835 strips but the volumes here (a few metres per cabin) make that premium negligible.
- Control protocol choice: DALI-2 DT8 is the building-integration standard in Europe (and what European lighting consultants/WELL assessors expect); Casambi (Bluetooth mesh) is the easiest retrofit/standalone option with an app and a Shenzhen controller supplier (Sunricher) already in its ecosystem; Zigbee/Tuya is cheapest for a self-contained cabin controller but is consumer-grade. For a self-service cabin with automatic reset, the cabin controller should drive scenes locally (no cloud dependency) and expose a simple interface (DALI or Casambi API) for building integration.
- Because Sunricher makes both Casambi and DALI DT8 controllers and is in Shenzhen, it is the natural single-source for a Guangzhou cabin integrator; LEDGEAR is a second DALI-2 DT8 driver source. European distributors (Simpex, Lumitronix/leds.de) are useful for reference specs and small-volume prototypes.

# Health-, sleep- and recovery add-ons for an unattended rest-cabin operator in Germany: evidence grading and DE/EU regulatory check

Research date: 21 October 2026. Scope: candidate add-on products and services for HAVN stations (Berlin pilot November 2026). Method note: all findings below come from web-search result pages (URLs given); direct retrieval of primary pages (BfR, BVL, EUR-Lex, gesetze-im-internet, PubMed, vendor sites) was blocked in the research environment, so wording of statutes, regulations and judgments is taken from secondary summaries and is marked **[verify at primary source]** where it matters. Nothing here is legal advice.

Evidence grades used: **strong** = consistent meta-analytic/RCT evidence in the relevant population; **moderate** = positive meta-analysis or several RCTs but small effects, heterogeneity or risk of bias; **weak** = single small trials, surrogate outcomes, or mixed results; **marketing** = no credible controlled evidence found for the claimed benefit.

---

## Key Question 1: Which five to ten add-ons have the best combination of real evidence, low regulatory risk, low operational burden in an unattended station, and willingness to pay? (includes the per-candidate catalogue)

### Takeaway
The best-balanced set is: (1) a "coffee nap" caffeine product bundled with the 20–30-minute nap slot; (2) disposable premium eye mask + earplug kits (already in the kit, upgrade to branded single-use/retail); (3) a magnesium or L-theanine sachet sold as a plain food supplement using only EFSA-authorised wording; (4) timed-light jet-lag protocols delivered through the cabin's own tunable lighting plus an in-app protocol (no new device, no claims beyond circadian "support"); (5) a licensed breathwork/NSDR audio library; (6) a premium sleep report/trend tracking from the existing radar sensor with explicit Art. 9 GDPR consent; (7) a referral path (not a sale) into statutory-insurer-reimbursed digital CBT-I (somnio, HelloBetter Schlafen) and ZPP-certified §20 SGB V stress/relaxation courses; (8) retail of Loop-type earplugs, Manta-type masks and blue-light glasses as sealed goods with no sleep-health claims; (9) weighted blankets only as sealed-cover rentals. Melatonin at ≤1 mg with the two authorised EFSA claims is legally possible but currently litigation-prone in Germany; CBD edibles, ashwagandha, any OTC medicine, loaned wearables and cold plunges should be excluded or deferred (see Key Question 5).

### Cited Findings

#### 1A. Consumables

**Melatonin**
- Evidence (moderate for sleep onset, moderate for jet lag, weak for chronic insomnia in adults): a 2022 meta-analysis of 34 RCTs (Salanitro et al., Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews) found benefits for sleep-onset latency mainly in children with neurodevelopmental disorders and adults with delayed sleep phase disorder, with tolerability no different from placebo — [PubMed 38888087 result set / Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/468887/1/1_s2.0_S0149763422002123_main.pdf); a 2024 dose-response meta-analysis of 26 trials (J Pineal Res) reported melatonin gradually reduces sleep-onset latency and increases total sleep time, peaking around 4 mg/day — [PubMed 38888087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38888087/); a 2022 Sleep Medicine Reviews analysis concluded melatonin "did not appear to be effective" for chronic insomnia in adults but might be in children/adolescents — [PubMed 36179487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36179487/); the Cochrane jet-lag review (summarised in CMAJ) found 0.5–5 mg taken at destination bedtime after crossing ≥5 time zones reduced jet-lag scores in most trials, more so eastward, and that occasional short-term use "appears to be safe" — [CMAJ summary of Cochrane CD001520](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/201/300/cmaj/2012/cmaj_184-17/pg1923.pdf); [Cochrane CD001520](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001520). IQWiG has an open ThemenCheck report on melatonin as a food supplement for sleep quality (protocol T24-03) — [IQWiG](https://www.iqwig.de/download/t24-03_schlafqualitaet-melatonin-als-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel_themencheck-berichtsprotokoll_v1-0.pdf).
- Authorised EU health claims: two claims are permitted for melatonin in food — easing the subjective feeling of jet lag and "Melatonin trägt dazu bei, die Einschlafzeit zu verkürzen"; products may carry the sleep-onset claim only when the daily dose of 1 mg is not exceeded and consumers are told to take it shortly before bed — [PTAheute 2022](https://ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2022/04/21/wie-viel-melatonin-in-nem-ist-erlaubt). Retailer labels across the EU state the conditions as "min. 1.0 mg melatonin … shortly before bedtime" for sleep onset and "minimum 0.5 mg … shortly before bedtime on the first day of travelling and a few days after arrival" for jet lag — [Scitec/Tonic](https://shop-tonic.acsone.eu/scitec-nutrition-night-melatonin-00018612); [Pharma Nord](https://www.pharmanord.eu/products/melatonine-3mg). **[verify exact wording in Regulation (EU) 432/2012 Annex at EUR-Lex]**
- Food-vs-medicine line in Germany: a Celle court classified "Sleepwell" 5 mg capsules as medicines (reasoning from Circadin's 2 mg approval); a Berlin court held 5 mg supplements are functional medicines needing AMG approval; a Frankfurt court held a 0.5 mg capsule taken 1–2×/day was not a functional medicine; no BGH ruling exists and no single threshold is settled, but at 1 mg daily "a food product is likely" — [SBS Legal](https://www.sbs-legal.de/blog/melatonin-arznei-oder-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel); [BVL FAQ on melatonin court proceedings](https://www.bvl.bund.de/DE/Arbeitsbereiche/01_Lebensmittel/04_AntragstellerUnternehmen/13_FAQ/FAQ_Melatonin/FAQ_Melatonin_node.html); [Apotheke adhoc](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/apothekenpraxis/melatonin-rechtslage-ungewiss-arzneimittel-oder-lebensmittel/). OLG Frankfurt (11.03.2021, 6 U 2/15) is cited for the view that melatonin "at least in low doses" is marketable as food — [PTAheute](https://ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2022/04/21/wie-viel-melatonin-in-nem-ist-erlaubt). Melatonin is listed in the AMVV, so every melatonin *medicine* is prescription-only — [SBS Legal](https://www.sbs-legal.de/blog/melatonin-arznei-oder-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel); [BfArM Sachverständigen-Ausschuss](https://www.bfarm.de/SharedDocs/Downloads/DE/Arzneimittel/Pharmakovigilanz/Gremien/Verschreibungspflicht/91Sitzung/anlage3.pdf?__blob=publicationFile).
- Safety position: BfR (2024) warns that doses of 1 mg/day and below may already cause unwanted effects according to existing studies, and that some supplements' recommended daily doses equal or exceed those of approved melatonin medicines — [BfR 2024 opinion](https://www.bfr.bund.de/stellungnahme/melatoninhaltige-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-bfr-weist-auf-moegliche-gesundheitsrisiken-hin); [BfR PDF](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/343/melatoninhaltige-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-bfr-weist-auf-moegliche-gesundheitsrisiken-hin-2024.pdf). Paediatricians warn against melatonin gummies for children — [Tagesspiegel Background](https://background.tagesspiegel.de/gesundheit-und-e-health/briefing/kinderaerzte-warnen-vor-melatonin-gummibaerchen).
- Claims litigation: OLG Frankfurt held "Erholsamer Schlaf"/"Restful Sleep" on melatonin gummies to be an unauthorised health claim for both the product and melatonin — [Lebensmittelklarheit](https://www.lebensmittelklarheit.de/news/werbung-erholsamer-schlaf-fuer-gummibaerchen-mit-melatonin-verboten) **[case number not found]**; OLG Koblenz (08.05.2023, 9 U 1947/22) found a melatonin + passionflower/California poppy/lemon balm/valerian product unlawfully advertised even though authorised melatonin wording was used, because the botanical sleep claims are "on hold" and the overall presentation was a sleep-promotion claim — [Preu Bohlig](https://preubohlig.de/newsletter/die-werbung-fuer-ein-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-mit-schlaffoerdernder-wirkung-ein-aktuelles-urteil-des-olg-koblenz-az-9-u-1947-22-vom-08-05-2023/); one commentary attributes the same facts to OLG Düsseldorf I-20 U 65/22, a conflict to resolve at source — [heilmittelwerberecht.net](https://www.heilmittelwerberecht.net/health-claims-verordnung).
- Rough pricing: no verified wholesale cost found; EU retail of 1 mg tablets/sprays is typically in the single-digit euro per pack range (see Gaps).

**Magnesium**
- Evidence (weak-to-moderate): Mah & Pitre 2021 pooled 3 placebo-controlled RCTs (151 older adults with insomnia): sleep-onset latency about 17 minutes shorter vs placebo, total sleep time +16 min not significant, all trials moderate-to-high risk of bias, low/very-low certainty; authors said the literature is too weak for firm recommendations — [PMC8053283](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8053283/); [PubMed 33865376](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865376/). A 2025 placebo-controlled trial of magnesium bisglycinate in healthy poor sleepers (Nature and Science of Sleep 2025;17:2027-2040) exists but results could not be retrieved — [search summary](https://pillo.care/blog/magnesium-for-sleep).
- Claims: EFSA's 2010 Article 13(1) opinion underlies authorised magnesium claims; labels lawfully use "contributes to a reduction of tiredness and fatigue", "normal psychological function", "normal functioning of the nervous system", "electrolyte balance", "normal muscle function" — [EFSA Journal 1807](https://efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1807); [Sunday.de label](https://www.sunday.de/en/magnesium-glycinate-capsules.html). A French regulatory summary gives the eligibility threshold as ≥56 mg magnesium per 100 g/100 ml or per single-portion package (which equals 15% of the 375 mg NRV) and notes rejected claims (blood pressure, gastric acidity, "resistance to psychological stress") — [Vidal](https://www.vidal.fr/parapharmacie/complements-alimentaires/magnesium.html). No sleep claim is authorised for magnesium (inference from the list above).

**Glycine**
- Evidence (weak): one 2007 trial in people with ongoing poor sleep (Sleep and Biological Rhythms) reported 3 g glycine before bed improved subjective sleep quality and efficiency, shortened PSG latency to sleep onset and slow-wave sleep, and reduced daytime sleepiness; no meta-analysis found — [Springer 2007](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/j.1479-8425.2007.00262.x).
- Claims: no authorised EFSA sleep claim found for glycine (gap; treat as unclaimable).

**L-theanine**
- Evidence (moderate but small effects): 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis in Sleep Medicine Reviews (19 articles, N=897, 18 in meta-analysis): improved subjective sleep-onset latency (SMD 0.15), daytime dysfunction (SMD 0.33) and overall subjective sleep quality (SMD 0.43); authors caution about lack of "pure" L-theanine studies and undefined dose/duration — [NutraIngredients 2025](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2025/03/31/review-explores-l-theanine-as-sleep-aid/); [nutrition-evidence record](https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/517433/the-effects-of-l-theanine-consumption-on-sleep-outcomes-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis).
- Claims: no authorised EFSA claim found for L-theanine (gap; treat as unclaimable; sell as plain "L-Theanin" ingredient without benefit statements).

**Valerian and hop teas**
- Evidence (weak/mixed): Shinjyo, Waddell & Green 2020 (60 studies, n=6,894; meta-analyses on 10 sleep-quality studies n=1,065) concluded valerian is "safe and effective" but inconsistent results likely stem from variable extract quality — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/a426af4ab8fe4bc4a662f7c38ffc31a1); the 2017 AASM guideline suggests clinicians not use valerian for insomnia, and a 2024 umbrella review found no benefit on objective insomnia measures — [MSD Manuals](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/dietary-supplements/valerian). A valerian-hop combination (Ze 91019) has trial evidence of superiority over placebo as a sleep-inducing *medicine* — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Axel-Brattstroem/publication/238724815_Eine_Baldrian-Hopfen-Kombination_Ze_91019_beim_Einsatz_als_Schlaf_anstossendes_Arzneimittel_Wissenschaftliche_Belege_fur_die_Uberlegenheit_gegenueber_Plazebo/links/53ff43970cf21ac8791d44d3/Eine-Baldrian-Hopfen-Kombination-Ze-91019-beim-Einsatz-als-Schlaf-anstossendes-Arzneimittel-Wissenschaftliche-Belege-fuer-die-Ueberlegenheit-gegenueber-Plazebo.pdf).
- Regulation: a German valerian-root tea sold with "treats restlessness and nervous sleep disorders" is a registered medicinal product (package leaflet) — [gebrauchs.info leaflet](https://api.gebrauchs.info/3e1be5cec653bb0dd9dde5b42fff3989i); an Austrian court required the authority to justify from what concentration valerian/hop extracts count as medicines — [Jusline](https://www.jusline.at/entscheidung/46132.pdf). Health claims for botanicals are "on hold": the ECJ (C-386/23, 30.04.2025) held botanical health claims may only continue under the national transitional regime, so sleep claims for valerian/hops/lemon balm in supplements are currently not usable in Germany — [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/urteile/zur-werbung-mit-gesundheitsbezogenen-angaben-fuer-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel); [Juravendis 2026](https://www.juravendis.de/ratgeber/blog/health-claims-verordnung-2026-hcvo-meno-botanicals-update).

**"Sleepy" drinks and gummies**
- Regulation: see melatonin gummies ruling above ("Restful Sleep" unlawful) — [Lebensmittelklarheit](https://www.lebensmittelklarheit.de/news/werbung-erholsamer-schlaf-fuer-gummibaerchen-mit-melatonin-verboten); even a product name can be a health claim (OLG interim ruling on "MenoGlück") — [Juravendis](https://www.juravendis.de/ratgeber/blog/health-claims-verordnung-2026-hcvo-meno-botanicals-update). Melatonin gummies attract paediatric warnings — [Heidelberg24](https://www.heidelberg24.de/welt/riskante-gummibaerchen-fuer-kinder-aerzte-warnen-eltern-vor-schlafmittel-trend-zr-93798263.html).

**CBD**
- Novel Food status: CBD foods still lack an EU authorisation; EFSA on 9 Feb 2026 set a provisional safe intake of ~2 mg/day for a 70 kg adult, only for ≥98%-pure CBD supplements without nanoparticles, and could not establish safety for under-25s, pregnant/lactating women or people on medication — [CIRS](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/food/efsa-establishes-provisional-safe-level-for-cannabidiol-cbd-intake-in-adults); [GMP-Compliance](https://www.gmp-compliance.org/gmp-news/efsa-sets-cannabidiol-cbd-safety-limit). EFSA's first positive safety opinion on a CBD novel-food application (Chanelle McCoy, ≥98% CBD in MCT oil, supplements only) came in September 2026; the Commission must still add it to the Union list — [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/09/21/important-turning-point-for-cbd-as-efsa-publishes-first-positive-safety-opinion/); [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/09/21/efsa-gives-positive-opinion-on-cbd/); a May 2026 opinion on a different CBD isolate (21 mg/day) concluded safety "cannot be established" — [LIH record](https://researchportal.lih.lu/en/publications/safety-of-cannabidiol-isolate-from-cannabis-sativa-l-as-a-novel-f/).
- THC: German sources conflict (0.3% for hemp products after the Cannabis Act vs the 0.2% EU cultivation figure); total THC is counted as THC + 0.877 × THCA — [Anwalt-Seiten](https://anwalt-seiten.de/cbd-in-deutschland-2026-rechtslage-thc-grenzwert); [cannabisregulations.ai](https://www.cannabisregulations.ai/country-legality/germany-cbd) **[conflicting; verify]**. Ingestible CBD remains the grey zone; many sellers declare products as cosmetics or aroma products to avoid Novel Food; earlier sources state selling CBD foods is prohibited with high fines — [High Level Genetics 2025](https://www.highlevelgenetics.com/post/rechtliche-%C3%BCbersicht-f%C3%BCr-cbd-reisende-stand-april-2025); [Greenocean 2026](https://www.greenoceancbd.de/cbd-in-deutschland-rechtliches-und-sicherheit).
- Evidence for sleep: not researched here (gap); regulatory status alone is disqualifying for 2026.

**Electrolyte and hydration drinks**
- Regulation: ordinary foods under LMIV; carbohydrate-electrolyte solutions have authorised claims ("enhance the absorption of water during physical exercise", "contribute to the maintenance of endurance performance") **[from general knowledge of Reg. 432/2012; not retrieved — verify]**. No sleep evidence; grade **marketing** for sleep, **moderate** for exercise hydration (claim-backed).

**Caffeine before a nap ("coffee nap") and caffeine products**
- Evidence (moderate, small studies): Reyner & Horne (1997) gave 12 sleep-restricted drivers decaf, caffeine (~150 mg) or caffeine followed by a ~15-minute nap during a 30-minute break; the caffeine+nap group had significantly fewer incidents in the following 2 hours of simulated driving (incidents 9% of placebo level vs 34% for caffeine alone) and reported higher alertness — [TRID record](https://trid.trb.org/View/457322); [SWOV](https://swov.nl/nl/publicatie/suppression-sleepiness-drivers-combination-caffiene-short-nap); Horne & Reyner found a break alone is ineffective and the maximum effect comes from combining nap and caffeine (Reyner & Horne 1999) — [korkortonline](https://korkortonline.se/en/theory-test/explanations/question/756). Evidence is small and not replicated at scale — [Bulletproof Musician summary](https://bulletproofmusician.com/are-coffee-naps-legit/).
- Safety/labelling: EFSA (2015): single doses up to 200 mg and daily intake up to 400 mg raise no safety concern for healthy adults; pregnant women ≤200 mg/day — [EUFIC](https://eufic.org/en/whats-in-food/article/efsa-opinion-on-the-safety-of-caffeine); [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2015/04/28/efsa-adopts-caffeine-opinion). Under the EU FIC/LMIV, drinks with added caffeine above 150 mg/l must carry a caffeine warning — [BeverageDaily](https://beveragedaily.com/Article/2015/01/16/efsa-400mg-of-caffeine-a-day-is-safe) **[exact Annex III wording "Erhöhter Koffeingehalt. Für Kinder und schwangere oder stillende Frauen nicht empfohlen" not retrieved — verify]**.
- Youth protection: Germany has no statutory sales ban of energy drinks to minors as of October 2026; a Green motion (Bundestag Drucksache 21/4591, 6 March 2026) and a Schleswig-Holstein Bundesrat initiative seek an under-16 ban; the federal nutrition minister is reported to oppose it; England bans sales of >150 mg/l drinks to under-16s from April 2027, explicitly including vending machines — [Bundestag Drs. 21/4591](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/045/2104591.pdf); [Ärzteblatt](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/themen/schleswig-holstein/schleswig-holstein-will-verbot-von-energydrinks-fur-jugendliche-aa684f58-5370-43b8-84f1-20ca6000f8cb); [inFranken](https://www.infranken.de/deutschland/verkaufsverbot-von-energy-drinks-an-kinder-die-ernaehrungsministerin-ist-dagegen-art-3523662); [SRF on UK](https://www.srf.ch/news/international/ab-april-2027-grossbritannien-verbietet-verkauf-von-energydrinks-an-kinder).

**Nootropics and adaptogens (ashwagandha)**
- Regulation/safety: BfR Mitteilung 39/2024 (10.09.2024) advises children, pregnant and breastfeeding women and people with current or past liver disease against ashwagandha, and "restraint" for everyone else because data do not allow a risk assessment; reported adverse effects include nausea, diarrhoea, rash and liver damage; ingredient contents vary strongly between products; BfR has advised against ashwagandha supplements since 2012/2013 — [Gelbe Liste](https://www.gelbe-liste.de/apotheke/sicherheitsbedenken-bfr-ashwagandha-schlafbeere); [Stiftung Warentest](https://www.test.de/Nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-Warnung-vor-Ashwagandha-6162426-0/); [Ernährungs Umschau](https://ernaehrungs-umschau.de/download/ashwagandha/); [Medscape](https://deutsch.medscape.com/artikelansicht/4914173). No German ban; freely sold as a supplement; Poland has set maximum levels — [ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/wissenschaft/ashwagandha-bfr-warnt-vor-risiken-fuer-kinder-und-schwangere/70049175). Botanical claims are on hold (ECJ C-386/23) — [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/urteile/zur-werbung-mit-gesundheitsbezogenen-angaben-fuer-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel). Evidence for sleep/stress not graded here (gap); regulatory and reputational risk is disqualifying.

**Light breakfast and recovery snacks**
- Regulation: ordinary food law (LMIV labelling; Reg. 852/2004 hygiene; vending rules in Key Question 3). No specific sleep evidence; grade **marketing** for sleep claims, but zero claim risk if sold as plain food.

#### 1B. Durable rentals and sales

**Blue-light-blocking glasses**
- Evidence (weak/inconclusive): Cochrane 2023 (Singh et al., CD013244; 17 trials, 5–156 participants each): sleep effects "indeterminate" — three trials (n=90) no difference in sleep quality, three (n=60) improvement; probably little or no effect on visual performance; search last run March 2022 — [Cochrane PDF](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013244.pub2/media/CDSR/CD013244/CD013244.pdf); [City St George's summary](https://www.citystgeorges.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/08/blue-light-filtering-spectacles-probably-no-difference-eye-strain-visual-performance-sleep-quality).
- Regulation: consumer product; any "improves sleep" statement is a UWG misleading-advertising risk given the Cochrane result (inference). Low hygiene burden if sold sealed, not loaned.

**Premium eye masks (Manta-type) and earplugs (Loop-type)**
- Evidence (moderate, ICU populations): 2021 meta-analysis (Karimi et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry; 35 studies, 2,687 critically ill adults) found eye masks and/or earplugs improved sleep quality and PSG total sleep time/efficiency — [Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2021.791342/full); [PMC8678458](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8678458/); a 2021 Journal of Advanced Nursing meta-analysis (13 RCTs, 797 ICU patients) found eye masks alone effective (ES 1.56), earplugs alone not (ES 0.07), combination largest (ES 2.08) — [PubMed 34096647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34096647/).
- Regulation: consumer goods (earplugs may be PPE if marketed for hearing protection — inference). Hygiene: single-use or personal-ownership items; Loop and Manta retail prices not retrievable (see Gaps).

**Weighted blankets**
- Evidence (moderate in psychiatric insomnia; weak otherwise): Ekholm, Spulber & Adler 2020 (J Clin Sleep Med 16(9):1567) randomised 120 psychiatric outpatients with insomnia to an 8 kg chain blanket vs 1.5 kg control for 4 weeks; insomnia severity (ISI) and daytime symptoms improved, supported by actigraphy; the blanket is patented/manufactured by Abilia and the lead author is a prescriber of weighted blankets (conflict of interest) — [PMC7970589](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7970589/); [Forbes Japan](https://forbesjapan.com/articles/detail/37748); [Abilia](https://abilia.com/node/11076). A 2020 systematic review (8 studies) found insufficient evidence for insomnia, possible benefit for anxiety — [PubMed 32204779](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32204779/); a 2024 scoping review reports more evidence in adults than children — [AJOT 2024](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.5014/ajot.2024.050676); 2024 meta-analysis in people with mental disorders (Zhao et al.) exists but pooled results were not retrieved — [PubMed 39447684](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39447684/); paediatric trials show no objective sleep benefit — [Cerebra 2023](https://cerebra.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Weighted-blanket-2023.pdf).
- Hygiene/liability: shared textile; needs a laundered removable cover per guest (inference); contraindications (respiratory/mobility limitations) are standard manufacturer warnings (inference, not retrieved).

**Cooling pillows, white-noise devices**
- No evidence retrieved in this research (gap). The cabin already has sound masking, which overlaps with white-noise devices (inference).

**Wearables loaned per stay (Oura, Whoop) or sold; HRV sensors**
- Oura for Business offers organisations anonymised group-level Sleep/Readiness/Activity reporting; 200+ customers — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-for-business/). Oura Ring 4 launched in Germany at €399 (glossy), €449 (brushed), €549 (gold/rose gold) plus €69.99/year subscription; Oura Ring 5 reportedly from €429 plus €5.99/month — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.com/Oura-Ring-4-mit-acht-Tagen-Laufzeit-und-praeziseren-Sensoren-ist-ab-sofort-im-deutschen-Handel-erhaeltlich.971836.0.html); [iPhone-Ticker](https://www.iphone-ticker.de/oura-und-ringconn-smarte-ringe-im-ausverkauf-280452/). No sources found on loan programmes or hygiene protocols for rings/straps (gap). Wearable sleep/heart-rate data are treated as Art. 9 GDPR health data requiring explicit, specific consent — [Thryve](https://www.thryve.health/blog/wearable-data-special-category-gdpr); [HBO Kennisbank 2021 paper](https://hbo-kennisbank.nl/details/sharekit_hh:oai:surfsharekit.nl:ee3bba3c-c3e9-4f64-b3f5-66cc0f3bb9bd).
- HRV biofeedback evidence (moderate for stress/anxiety): Goessl, Curtiss & Hofmann 2017 (24 studies, 484 participants): within-group g = 0.81, vs control g = 0.83; more well-controlled studies needed — [Cambridge/Psychological Medicine](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/effect-of-heart-rate-variability-biofeedback-training-on-stress-and-anxiety-a-metaanalysis/A839E9C968E54774DF5C8FB186764EF0); [BU OpenBU](https://open.bu.edu/handle/2144/26911).

**Light-therapy glasses for jet lag (Luminette, Ayo)**

### Inferences
- Ranking logic: items with authorised claims or no claims needed (caffeine, plain foods, masks/earplugs, lighting protocols, audio, data reports) carry near-zero claim risk, no hygiene sharing and no supervision need; melatonin ≤1 mg is legally arguable but the BfR warning plus the OLG Koblenz/Frankfurt line and open IQWiG review make it the single most litigable consumable.
- Rough unit economics (estimates, **not sourced**): single-serve supplement sachets/tablets typically cost well under €1 wholesale and retail €2–5; sealed earplug/eye-mask retail kits €5–15 (premium brands €25–50); weighted-blanket rental needs ~€80–200 capital plus laundering; Luminette-class devices ~€180–230 retail make loan-per-stay uneconomic versus affiliate sale; a 90-day DiGA is worth €225–241 to the manufacturer, so a referral partnership would be the only monetisable element (if lawful).
- The cabin's own tunable lighting can deliver a timed-light jet-lag protocol without any additional device, avoiding MDR exposure as long as the app frames it as circadian "comfort" and not treatment.
- Loaned rings/straps are high-touch, high-value, and generate Art. 9 data under the manufacturer's account structure; selling or affiliate-linking is lower burden than loaning.

## Key Question 2: What exactly may a German operator say about sleep and health benefits of consumables and devices without breaching HWG, UWG, the Health Claims Regulation (EC) 1924/2006 or the MDR?

### Takeaway
For foods and supplements, only the authorised claim wording may be used (melatonin: "trägt dazu bei, die Einschlafzeit zu verkürzen" at ≥1 mg before bed; jet-lag claim at ≥0.5 mg; magnesium: tiredness/fatigue, psychological and nervous-system function), botanical sleep claims are unusable while "on hold", and the overall presentation (product name, imagery, "restful sleep") is judged as a whole; for devices, a non-medical product must not be presented as preventing, diagnosing or treating disease, and even for CE medical devices advertising to laypeople must not be misleading (§3 HWG) and must respect §11/§12 HWG.

### Cited Findings
- Health claims on supplements are prohibited unless authorised; after ECJ C-386/23 (30.04.2025) botanical claims may only continue under the member state's transitional regime, so sleep claims for plant substances are currently not usable in German supplement advertising — [vzbv](https://www.vzbv.de/urteile/zur-werbung-mit-gesundheitsbezogenen-angaben-fuer-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel); [Juravendis](https://www.juravendis.de/ratgeber/blog/health-claims-verordnung-2026-hcvo-meno-botanicals-update).
- OLG Koblenz 9 U 1947/22: using authorised melatonin wording does not save a product whose overall presentation (name, botanicals) promises sleep promotion — [Preu Bohlig](https://preubohlig.de/newsletter/die-werbung-fuer-ein-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-mit-schlaffoerdernder-wirkung-ein-aktuelles-urteil-des-olg-koblenz-az-9-u-1947-22-vom-08-05-2023/); "Für alle, die sich nach einer sanften Hilfe für einen guten Schlaf sehnen" was held to breach the HCVO — [heilmittelwerberecht.net](https://www.heilmittelwerberecht.net/health-claims-verordnung).
- "Erholsamer Schlaf"/"Restful Sleep" on melatonin gummies: unauthorised health claim — [Lebensmittelklarheit](https://www.lebensmittelklarheit.de/news/werbung-erholsamer-schlaf-fuer-gummibaerchen-mit-melatonin-verboten). A product name can itself be a health claim ("MenoGlück") — [Juravendis](https://www.juravendis.de/ratgeber/blog/health-claims-verordnung-2026-hcvo-meno-botanicals-update). "Anti-Kater" on a supplement was stopped by OLG Frankfurt (02.12.2024, 6 U 124/24) — [MTR Legal](https://www.mtrlegal.com/werbung-fuer-lebensmittel-mit-anti-kater-claim-vom-olg-gestoppt/).
- Permitted melatonin wording and conditions (1 mg sleep onset; 0.5 mg jet lag) — [PTAheute](https://ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2022/04/21/wie-viel-melatonin-in-nem-ist-erlaubt); [Pharma Nord label](https://www.pharmanord.eu/products/melatonine-3mg).
- Permitted magnesium wording and threshold — [Vidal](https://www.vidal.fr/parapharmacie/complements-alimentaires/magnesium.html); [EFSA Journal 1807](https://efsa.europa.eu/en/efsajournal/pub/1807).
- HWG: §3 (misleading) is the central provision; §11 breaches make advertising unlawful regardless; §12 restricts lay advertising only for the Annex diseases (notifiable infections, malignant neoplasms, addictions, pregnancy complications); §12 was last amended 26.06.2026 (IfSG testing exception) — [RA Himburg](https://www.ra-himburg-berlin.de/wettbewerbsrecht/faq/1659-sicher-werben-im-gesundheitsmarkt-heilmittelwerbung-in-der-praxis.html); [gesetze-im-internet §12](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/heilmwerbg/__12.html); [HWG Anlage](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/heilmwerbg/anlage.html); [Ratgeberrecht](https://www.ratgeberrecht.eu/aktuell/gesundheitsbezogene-werbung-2/).
- Devices: Beurer daylight lamps (class IIa, notified body) phrase their purpose as "ausgleichen"/"lindern" of winter-type affective disorders, mood swings and sleep problems, with listed contraindications — [Beurer TL30G IFU](https://res.cloudinary.com/beurer/image/upload/v1787031206/stibo-live/60805_TL30G_2026-07-22_10_IM1_BEU_MDR.pdf); [testundtipps.de](https://testundtipps.de/familie-haustiere/koerperpflege/tageslichtlampe/lichttherapie-krankenkasse). Withings instructs users with apnoea signals to consult a doctor — [Withings FAQ](https://support.withings.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000038707-Sleep-Analyzer-EU-ROW-Frequently-asked-questions).
- Blue-light glasses: Cochrane 2023 found no clear sleep benefit, so a sleep claim would be unsupported — [Cochrane](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD013244.pub2/media/CDSR/CD013244/CD013244.pdf).

### Inferences
- Safe-harbour language for HAVN (inference from the above): describe the *environment and routine* ("dark, quiet, cool cabin; dawn simulation; a 25-minute nap window"), quote only authorised nutrient claims verbatim with their dose conditions, never pair a product with "Schlaf", "erholsam", "Insomnie", "Stress abbauen" or disease names, and label the sleep report as a "Komfort-/Wellness-Auswertung, kein Medizinprodukt, keine Diagnose".
- For non-medical devices (red-light panel, glasses, sound devices), any statement like "treats", "prevents", "jet lag therapy", "reduces apnoea" would make the product a presentation-medical-device under MDR Art. 2(1) and trigger HWG; the HWG Annex does not list sleep disorders, but §3 HWG and UWG §5 still apply to unsupported effect claims (legal inference, not sourced).
- Caffeine: "Koffein trägt zu einer Erhöhung der Aufmerksamkeit/Konzentration bei" is widely used in the EU but the Commission never formally adopted the caffeine claims; treat as **unverified** and prefer factual dosing statements (e.g., "enthält 100 mg Koffein") — inference from general knowledge.

### Gaps
- EU Register wording for melatonin/magnesium/caffeine not retrieved at source.
- No authority guidance found on advertising of non-medical "wellness" sleep devices in Germany (DSK/Länder) beyond general HWG commentary.

## Key Question 3: What are the rules for selling food supplements and over-the-counter products through vending machines or an app in Germany (Lebensmittelrecht, Jugendschutz, pharmacy monopoly)?

### Takeaway
Foods and food supplements may be sold from vending machines and apps if the operator registers as a food business (Reg. 852/2004 Art. 6), meets Annex II Chapter III hygiene rules, labels per LMIV/NemV and provides the mandatory information at the point of sale; medicines may not be sold by vending machine or self-service at all (§52 AMG), and even pharmacy-free ("freiverkäufliche") medicines require continuous presence of a person with §50 AMG expertise, which an unattended station cannot provide; there is currently no age limit for caffeine/energy drinks in Germany, but one is under debate.

### Cited Findings
- Hygiene: Annex II Chapter III of Reg. (EC) 852/2004 applies to vending machines; machines must be sited, built and maintained to avoid contamination, food-contact surfaces clean and disinfectable, cold chain maintained (Art. 4(3)(c)); an in-house self-control system with temperature checks is required — [LGL Baden-Württemberg Merkblatt](https://lkl.lgl-bw.de/export/sites/lkl/Projekte/Galerien/Dokumente/Merkblatt-Lebensmittelautomaten.pdf); [Stadt Mainz Merkblatt](https://www.mainz.de/vv/medien/internet/Merkblatt-Automaten.pdf); [Lübeck Merkblatt](https://www.luebeck.de/files/rathaus/verwaltung/Gesundheitsamt/Dokument%20-%20Merkblatt%20zur%20Lebensmittelhygiene%20bei%20Verkaufsautomaten.pdf).
- Registration: vending must be notified to the food-control authority responsible for the machine's location, even by an already-registered business (Art. 6 Reg. 852/2004); §14(3) GewO requires name and address on the machine — [Stadt Mainz](https://www.mainz.de/vv/medien/internet/Merkblatt-Automaten.pdf).
- Supplements: only "Nahrungsergänzungsmittel" as designation; mandatory nutrient categories, recommended daily portion, "do not exceed", "not a substitute for a varied diet", "keep out of reach of small children"; notification duty under §5 NemV (BVL), no pre-market approval; LMIV applies; health claims only per Reg. 1924/2006 — [Verbraucherzentrale](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.de/wissen/lebensmittel/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-was-das-gesetz-erlaubt-4988); [Lebensmittelverband](https://www.lebensmittelverband.de/de/lebensmittel/nahrungsergaenzungsmittel/nem-gesetzliche-regelungen); [BVL NEM](https://www.bvl.bund.de/DE/Arbeitsbereiche/01_Lebensmittel/04_AntragstellerUnternehmen/03_NEM/lm_nahrungsErgMittel_node.html); online product descriptions must carry the same information — [IT-Recht Kanzlei](https://www.it-recht-kanzlei.de/rechtssichere-verkauf-nahrungserg%C3%A4nzungsmitteln.html); [Händlerbund](https://www.haendlerbund.de/de/ratgeber/branchen/3829-verkauf-von-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel).
- Medicines: §52 AMG prohibits placing medicines on the market via vending machines or other self-service; §52(3) allows self-service only for medicines released for sale outside pharmacies and only if a person with §50 expertise is available; OVG Lüneburg (2012) confirmed continuous presence during opening hours — [lxgesetze §52 AMG](https://lxgesetze.de/amg/52); [IWW on OVG Lüneburg](https://www.iww.de/ah/recht/arzneimittelversorgung-selbstbedienungsverbot-auch-fuer-den-arzneimittelverkauf-ausserhalb-von-apotheken-bestaetigt-f60694); [Cochem-Zell Merkblatt AMG](https://www.cochem-zell.de/themen/gesundheit-hygiene/formulare-gesundheitsamt/merkblatt-amg-ga.pdf?cid=u4j); retail of freiverkäufliche medicines must be notified and requires §50 Sachkenntnis — [Heidelberg service portal](https://www.heidelberg.de/,Lde/-/Verfahrensbeschreibung/einzelhandel-mit-freiverkaeuflichen-arzneimitteln-anzeigen/vbid6024025).
- Melatonin medicines are prescription-only (AMVV) and melatonin supplements >~1–2 mg risk classification as functional medicines (see KQ1) — [SBS Legal](https://www.sbs-legal.de/blog/melatonin-arznei-oder-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel).
- Jugendschutz: no German sales ban on energy drinks to minors yet; proposals pending (Bundestag Drs. 21/4591; Schleswig-Holstein) — [Bundestag](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/045/2104591.pdf); [Lebensmittelpraxis](https://lebensmittelpraxis.de/handel-aktuell/47406-schleswig-holstein-cdu-will-energydrinks-fuer-unter-16-jaehrige-verbieten.html). Caffeine-drink warning labelling >150 mg/l — [BeverageDaily](https://beveragedaily.com/Article/2015/01/16/efsa-400mg-of-caffeine-a-day-is-safe).
- Self-test IVDs: §3(4) MPAV limits pathogen IVDs (§24 IfSG list) to professional circles; nutrient tests are outside that wording (inference) — [BMG MPAV amendment](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/3_Downloads/Gesetze_und_Verordnungen/GuV/H/HIV-Selbsttest-Aenderung_MPAV.pdf).

### Inferences
- App-based sale with in-cabin pickup is a distance contract (BGB §312c) with information and withdrawal duties, but sealed hygiene goods and perishable foods are exempt from withdrawal (BGB §312g(2)) — general legal knowledge, **unverified in this research**.
- Because no §50-qualified person is present, HAVN cannot sell *any* medicine (including valerian/hop *medicinal* teas, doxylamine/diphenhydramine sleep aids, or prescription melatonin); teas must be sourced and labelled as foods without therapeutic claims.
- Age verification in the app (18+ account) would pre-empt a future energy-drink age limit and the HWG/UWG sensitivity around caffeine for minors.

### Gaps
- No source on whether an app-triggered in-cabin dispense counts as "Fernabsatz" or on-site sale for LMIV pre-contractual information (Art. 14 LMIV).
- Current text of §52 AMG and NemV not retrieved at primary source.

## Key Question 4: How do statutory health insurers reimburse or subsidise sleep-related prevention (§20 SGB V courses, bonus programmes, DiGA) and could a cabin operator plug into that?

### Takeaway
Three channels exist: ZPP-certified §20 SGB V prevention courses (stress/relaxation field; most Kassen reimburse two courses a year, typically 80–100% up to a cap), insurer bonus programmes (individual rules, often €100–200+/year, increasingly accepting app-based proof), and DiGA (somnio €224.99, HelloBetter Schlafen €241.25 per 90 days, prescription or direct Kasse approval). A cabin operator can only plug in by (a) hosting or co-marketing a ZPP-certified course delivered by a qualified trainer, (b) signposting guests to DiGA/Kasse routes without diagnosing, or (c) negotiating recognition in a Kasse bonus programme; no precedent of a rest-cabin operator doing so was found.

### Cited Findings
- §20 SGB V: the ZPP checks trainer qualifications and course concepts against the Leitfaden Prävention with four fields (exercise, nutrition, stress/resource management, substance use); criteria version dated 06.01.2026; trainers need a state-recognised degree/vocational qualification plus field-specific training; already-certified programmes can be used by qualified providers without re-certification; certification is free — [GKV-Spitzenverband Kriterien 06.01.2026](https://www.gkv-spitzenverband.de/media/dokumente/krankenversicherung_1/praevention__selbsthilfe__beratung/praevention/praevention_leitfaden/Kriterien_zur_Zertifizierung_von_Praeventionskursangeboten_gemaess_Kapitel_5_Stand_06.01.2026.pdf); [AHAB Akademie FAQ](https://www.ahab-akademie.de/faq/praeventionskurse-nach-%C2%A720-sgb-v-zertifizieren/); [BSA Akademie](https://www.bsa-akademie.de/lehrgaenge/fitness-gruppentraining/praeventionsangebote-nach-20-sgb-v); [Bornholdt Lee](https://www.bornholdtlee.de/wissen/zpp-zentrale-pruefstelle-praevention-zertifizierung/).
- Reimbursement: most Kassen reimburse two courses per year, between €150 and €1,200 depending on the Kasse; digital live or self-guided programmes qualify if ZPP-certified — [betanet](https://www.betanet.de/bonusprogramme.html); [praeventionskurse-suchen.de](https://praeventionskurse-suchen.de/was-ist-ein-praeventionskurs-2/). Example: a ZPP-certified online sleep course (Blackroll) priced €149 with user-reported TK reimbursement of €120; others report 60–67% — [mydealz discussion](https://www.mydealz.de/diskussion/krankenkassen-schlafkurs-und-blackroll-kissen-im-wert-von-79eur-geschenkt-2676624) (user reports, not official).
- Bonus programmes: each Kasse sets its own rules; rewards include check-ups, vaccinations, sport, nutrition and stress-management activities; some advertise €100–200+ per year; proof is increasingly digital (app upload); deadlines apply — [Verbraucherzentrale NRW](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.nrw/wissen/gesundheit-pflege/krankenversicherung/bonusprogramme-der-krankenkassen-12218); [ÖKO-TEST](https://www.oekotest.de/geld-versicherungen/bonusprogramm-der-krankenkasse-viele-kennen-den-finanziellen-vorteil-nicht_16252_1.html); some programmes part-fund smartwatches — [netzwelt](https://www.netzwelt.de/news/257503-7143-schritte-tag-bringen-15-euro-so-holt-eurer-smartwatch-geld-krankenkasse.html); [GIGA on Barmer/Apple Watch](https://www.giga.de/sparen/bezahlt-die-barmer-die-apple-watch-so-funktioniert-das-bonusprogramm--01K9PSPTAQHMPZSHYRK37KQ22K).
- Employer channel: up to €600 per employee per year tax-free for certified prevention (§3 Nr. 34 EStG) is cited by providers — [gesundheitsmanagement24](https://www.gesundheitsmanagement24.de/zpp-zertifizierte-praeventionskurse/).
- DiGA: see KQ1 — [BfArM 00508](https://diga.bfarm.de/de/verzeichnis/00508); [diga-verzeichnis.de HelloBetter](https://www.diga-verzeichnis.de/diga/hellobetter-schlafen); [BAS Rundschreiben](https://www.bundesamtsozialesicherung.de/fileadmin/redaktion/Krankenversicherung/Rundschreiben/20230616RundschreibenDIGA.pdf).

### Inferences
- The §20 "Stress- und Ressourcenmanagement" field (relaxation, mindfulness-type programmes) is the realistic certification path; a stand-alone "sleep" course appears possible only if framed within that field (no source confirmed a sleep-specific category).
- A ZPP course must be delivered by a qualified human trainer under a certified concept; HAVN could act as venue/distribution partner (e.g., a course provider's 8-week online programme with cabin sessions), not as the certified provider itself unless it employs qualified trainers.
- DiGA manufacturers (mementor/somnio, HelloBetter) run B2B channels for employers/insurers; a cabin operator could at most provide informational signposting; paying referral fees for prescriptions would raise §31 HWG/§299a StGB-type concerns (legal inference, unverified).

### Gaps
- No Kasse bonus-programme terms retrieved that name sleep, nap or rest services; no evidence that any Kasse recognises commercial rest facilities.
- No documentation of DiGA referral partnerships with non-medical businesses.

## Key Question 5: Which items create hygiene or liability problems when shared between guests, and what should be excluded for safety or legal reasons?

### Takeaway
Exclude medicines of any kind (vending/self-service ban), CBD edibles (Novel Food, THC uncertainty), ashwagandha (BfR warning), melatonin above 1 mg and any melatonin product aimed at or attractive to minors, shared compression boots and loaned wearables without a cleaning/consent regime, and cold plunges or saunas until a hygiene (IfSG §37/DIN 19643, Legionella) and unattended-use safety concept exists; share only items with a laundered or disposable interface (blankets with covers, sealed masks/earplugs).

### Cited Findings
- Medicines: vending/self-service ban and §50 expertise requirement — [lxgesetze §52 AMG](https://lxgesetze.de/amg/52); [IWW](https://www.iww.de/ah/recht/arzneimittelversorgung-selbstbedienungsverbot-auch-fuer-den-arzneimittelverkauf-ausserhalb-von-apotheken-bestaetigt-f60694).
- Melatonin: BfR warns of effects at ≤1 mg and doses matching prescription medicines; paediatric warnings on gummies — [BfR](https://www.bfr.bund.de/stellungnahme/melatoninhaltige-nahrungsergaenzungsmittel-bfr-weist-auf-moegliche-gesundheitsrisiken-hin); [Tagesspiegel Background](https://background.tagesspiegel.de/gesundheit-und-e-health/briefing/kinderaerzte-warnen-vor-melatonin-gummibaerchen).
- CBD: no EU authorisation; EFSA provisional 2 mg/day and exclusions for under-25s, pregnancy, medication — [CIRS](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/food/efsa-establishes-provisional-safe-level-for-cannabidiol-cbd-intake-in-adults).
- Ashwagandha: BfR 39/2024 liver-injury reports and advice of restraint — [Gelbe Liste](https://www.gelbe-liste.de/apotheke/sicherheitsbedenken-bfr-ashwagandha-schlafbeere).
- Compression boots: manufacturer warns against unsupervised use by incapacitated persons; DVT contraindication — [Normatec 3 manual](https://pickleballcentral.com/content/Normatec3_UserManual.pdf).
- Cold plunge/basins: disinfection required and DIN 19643/UBA supervision under §37(2) IfSG; monthly microbiology (Legionella, Pseudomonas, E. coli) in state guidance — [LAGuS MV](https://www.lagus.mv-regierung.de/serviceassistent/download?id=1665940); [UBA](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/themen/wasser/schwimmen-baden/regelwerke-normen).
- Showers: Legionella testing duty for large installations (3-yearly commercial, annual public) — [Kreis Coesfeld](https://www.kreis-coesfeld.de/media/default/53-Gesundheitsamt/Downloads/FD4_Umwelt-_und_Infektionsschutz/Merkblatt_Trinkwasseruntersuchung_auf_Legionellen.pdf).
- Wearable/sleep data: Art. 9 GDPR, explicit consent, no legitimate-interest basis — [Thryve](https://www.thryve.health/blog/wearable-data-special-category-gdpr).
- Blood draws/lab tests: physician reservation upheld for Heilpraktiker Eigenblut draws (BVerfG 1 BvR 2324/24); pathogen self-tests restricted (MPAV §3(4)) — [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/bverfg-1bvr232424-arzt-vorbehalt-eigenblut-therapie-heilpraktiker); [BMG MPAV](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/fileadmin/Dateien/3_Downloads/Gesetze_und_Verordnungen/GuV/H/HIV-Selbsttest-Aenderung_MPAV.pdf).
- Massage/therapy: Heilkunde permit boundary (§1 HeilprG) — [Kanzlei Herfurtner](https://kanzlei-herfurtner.de/heilpraktikergesetz/).

### Inferences
- Shared-interface hierarchy (inference): single-use (masks, earplugs, tea, sachets) → laundered textile per guest (blanket cover, towel) → wipeable hard surface (massage chair, BP cuff) → skin-contact electronics (rings, straps, boots: highest burden) → water systems (plunge, sauna, steam: regulatory inspection regime).
- Unattended infrared sauna and cold plunge create thermal-stress/syncope and drowning-type risks with no staff; the Normatec unsupervised-use warning illustrates how manufacturers shift that liability to operators; a remote emergency button, session timer and radar presence detection would be minimum mitigations (inference).
- Blood tests: a lawful model would be affiliate sale of a physician-supervised home-sampling kit (ZAVA-type) or a partner physician's practice; HAVN must not draw blood, interpret results or advertise disease detection.

### Gaps
- No official guidance found on unattended sauna/cold-plunge operation, on whether small cold tubs are "Badebecken", or on wearable cleaning standards.
- EU MDR classification of Normatec-type boots and of Luminette/Ayo glasses not retrieved.

# B2B, membership and partnership add-on models for HAVN rest cabins

Scope: corporate/employer models, travel and mobility partners, health-system partners, real-estate hosts, consumer bundles in adjacent categories, and data/research partnerships, Germany first. Research date: 21 October 2026. Method note: web search only; direct page fetches (WebFetch/curl) were blocked by the session proxy, so findings rest on search-result excerpts of the cited pages rather than full-text reads. Where a figure comes from a secondary or aggregator site, or where the page is old, this is flagged inline. Prices are in EUR unless the source only gives USD/GBP.

## KQ1. Which B2B models have precedents with real revenue, and what are the typical economics?

### Takeaway
Three B2B revenue models have proven precedents: (1) per-visit payouts from corporate-wellness aggregators (Wellhub, Urban Sports Club, EGYM Wellpass, Hansefit), where the venue is paid per check-in and the aggregator charges the employer a per-employee monthly fee of roughly EUR 30-60; (2) corporate pod leases/"pod as a service" (MetroNaps/Restworks, Framery Subscribed, Napcabs' pivot), priced as fixed monthly rentals or USD 10-19k purchases; and (3) employer-paid digital sleep/mind programmes (Sleepio, Calm, Headspace) at roughly USD 14-70 per employee per year. Per-check-in rates are never published; the only data points are single-digit to low double-digit EUR per visit.

### Inferences
- The aggregator channel is the fastest route to employer money with zero sales effort per company: HAVN would be listed as a "wellness" venue (as spas and massage providers already are) and paid per check-in. Given the EUR 29/2h consumer price and published anecdotes (EUR 6.50-8 per check-in on EUR 10-15 list prices), a realistic aggregator payout for a 2-hour stay is likely EUR 10-20, i.e. a 30-65% discount to list, and aggregators will almost certainly cap usage per month the way USC caps massages (1-2/month) and Wellhub caps spa visits (5/month).
- Corporate pod leasing precedents (EnergyPod USD 795/month in 2013; Framery about GBP 310/month) suggest a "HAVN cabin in your building" lease could be priced at several hundred EUR per cabin per month, plus service, and compared favourably with a USD 10-19k capex purchase.
- Employer digital sleep programmes are cheap (USD 14-70 per employee per year), so a HAVN employer credit package would be positioned against that benchmark; a seat priced at the Hansefit level (EUR 49-59 per employee per month) is only plausible if it includes meaningful usage (e.g. 2-4 stays per month).

### Gaps
- No aggregator (Wellhub, USC, Wellpass, Hansefit) publishes per-check-in rates; the only figures are anecdotal and 5+ years old. Partner-contract terms must be obtained directly.
- Restworks' current rental/subscription prices and named clients were not retrievable (site fetch blocked).
- No German corporate installation of commercial nap pods with disclosed cost (beyond IKEA's in-house Schlafkojen) was found.
- SleepScore Labs B2B pricing and current Sleepio/Big Health employer pricing were not found.

## KQ2. Which German legal or tax rules create a budget employers can spend on rest cabins, and what certification would HAVN need?

### Takeaway
Four employer budget mechanisms are relevant: §3 Nr. 34 EStG (EUR 600 per employee per year tax-free, but only for measures meeting §§20/20b SGB V criteria, in practice ZPP-certified courses); the EUR 50/month Sachbezug (voucher/card benefit, no certification needed); the Erholungsbeihilfe (EUR 156 per employee per year at 25% flat tax, earmarked for recreation); and the Arbeitsstättenverordnung/ASR A4.2 and §9 MuSchG duties to provide break rooms and lying-down facilities. A pure cabin stay is unlikely to qualify under §3 Nr. 34 unless packaged into a ZPP-certified stress/sleep course; the Sachbezug and Erholungsbeihilfe routes have no certification hurdle.

### Cited Findings

§3 Nr. 34 EStG (Betriebliche Gesundheitsförderung)
- Employers may spend up to EUR 600 per employee per calendar year tax-free; it is a Freibetrag (only the excess is taxable), unchanged since 2020 with no adjustment for 2026 — [lofino.de](https://www.lofino.de/blog/gesundheitsbonus-steuerfrei-voraussetzungen/), [prevencer.de](https://prevencer.de/gesundheitskurse-fuer-mitarbeiter/), [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/arbeitsschutz/gesundheit-umwelt/600-euro-pro-beschaeftigten-steuerfrei-fuer-die-bgf_94_699106.html)
- Eligible measures must meet the requirements of §§20 and 20b SGB V; in practice this means proof via the Zentrale Prüfstelle Prävention (ZPP) — [salfy.de](https://salfy.de/betriebliche-gesundheitsfoerderung/), [prevencer.de](https://prevencer.de/gesundheitskurse-fuer-mitarbeiter/)
- The benefit must be granted in addition to wages already owed (Zusätzlichkeit) — [Haufe Personal](https://www.haufe.de/personal/entgelt/gesundheitsfoerderung-durch-den-arbeitgeber_78_509602.html)
- An employer-run in-house course must be identical to the certified course and the certificate must be in the name of the course leader and valid at course start — [Haufe Personal](https://www.haufe.de/personal/entgelt/gesundheitsfoerderung-durch-den-arbeitgeber_78_509602.html)
- Attendance: 80% of units for in-person courses, 100% for online courses — [prevencer.de](https://prevencer.de/gesundheitskurse-fuer-mitarbeiter/)
- Lump-sum subsidies without individual proof do not qualify; receipts must be assigned to the payroll account — [lofino.de](https://www.lofino.de/blog/gesundheitsbonus-steuerfrei-voraussetzungen/)
- The ZPP alone issues the "Deutscher Standard Prävention" seal; certification follows the current GKV-Spitzenverband Leitfaden Prävention — [Akademie Sport Gesundheit](https://www.akademie-sport-gesundheit.de/faq/primaerpraevention.html), [Bornholdt Lee](https://www.bornholdtlee.de/wissen/zpp-zentrale-pruefstelle-praevention-zertifizierung/)
- The current Leitfaden Prävention is the version of December 2025 — [GKV-Spitzenverband Leitfaden Prävention 2025 (PDF)](https://www.gkv-spitzenverband.de/media/dokumente/krankenversicherung_1/praevention__selbsthilfe__beratung/praevention/praevention_leitfaden/20251218_Leitfaden_Pravention_2025_barrierefrei.pdf); the relevant field is "Stressbewältigung"/"Stress- und Ressourcenmanagement", with provider examples including relaxation techniques and sleep quality — [praeventionskurse-suchen.de](https://praeventionskurse-suchen.de/was-ist-ein-praeventionskurs-2/); a simplified course-leader certification exists for Progressive Relaxation and Autogenic Training in that field (BDP document on the Dec 2023 version) — [BDP (PDF)](https://www.bdp-verband.de/fileadmin/user_upload/BDP/verband/Untergliederungen/Sektionen/Gesundheits-und-Umweltpsychologie/Gesundheitspsychologie/Beruf/GKV-Praevention-Surma.pdf)
- Insurer reimbursement of certified courses (example TK): 80% of the course cost, max EUR 150 per course, two courses per calendar year; TK gives no direct gym subsidy — [fragfitty.de](https://fragfitty.de/artikel/fitnessstudio-krankenkasse-zuschuss)

Sachbezug (EUR 50/month) and Erholungsbeihilfe
- The tax-free Sachbezug limit is EUR 50 per month (since 1 Jan 2022); it must be granted on top of wages, cannot be funded by salary conversion (since 2020) and cannot be paid in cash; voucher cards must be restricted to goods/services and meet ZAG criteria — [drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/sachbezugskarten-vergleich/), [Deutsche Handwerks Zeitung](https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/gehaltsextras-7-neue-spielregeln-fuer-gutscheine/150/32554/98945), [ETL](https://www.etl.de/aktuelles/gutscheine-und-gutscheinkarten-2025/)
- The BMF letter of 15 March 2022 defines the "limited circle of acceptance points"; a card issuer's cooperation with specific acceptance partners qualifies, whereas an Amazon voucher is treated as cash wages — [werbezeichen.de](https://www.werbezeichen.de/blog/sachbezuge-geldwerte-vorteile-welche-steuerlichen-regeln-arbeitgeber-beachten-mussen)
- Edenred describes itself as market leader for Sachbezug solutions with around 7,000 acceptance points depending on region — [Edenred press release (PDF)](https://www.edenred.de/fileadmin/user_upload/content/Edenred_PM_IPSOS_Studie_50-Euro-Sachbezug.pdf)
- Erholungsbeihilfe (§40 Abs. 2 EStG): employer may flat-tax at 25% up to EUR 156 per employee, EUR 104 per spouse and EUR 52 per child per calendar year, provided the employer ensures the money is used for recreation; social-insurance free within these limits — [factorialhr.de](https://factorialhr.de/blog/erholungsbeihilfe/), [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/erholungsbeihilfen-entgelt-HI2826454@HI2721049.html), [buhl.de](https://www.buhl.de/steuernsparen/steuerfreie-erholungsbeihilfe)

Arbeitsstättenverordnung, ASR A4.2 and Mutterschutz
- ArbStättV Anhang 4.2: a Pausenraum or Pausenbereich must be provided for more than ten employees, or regardless of headcount when safety or health reasons require it (heat, cold, dust, hazardous substances, or areas to which third parties such as customers have access); headcount includes temporary workers simultaneously present, excluding part-timers without break entitlement and predominantly field staff; the duty lapses for office workplaces with equivalent recovery possibilities — [KomNet 30410](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/30410), [KomNet 1946](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/1946), [Forum Verlag on ASR A4.2](https://www.forum-verlag.com/fachwissen/arbeitsschutz/asr-a4-2/)
- ASR A4.2 specifies a minimum floor area of 6.00 m² for a Pausenraum and defines Bereitschaftsräume as rooms for stays during on-call/standby time, to be equipped accordingly — [umwelt-online ASR A4.2](https://umwelt-online.de/recht/arbeitss/arbst.vo/arbst.rl/a4_2.htm), [Forum Verlag](https://www.forum-verlag.com/fachwissen/arbeitsschutz/asr-a4-2/)
- Pregnant and nursing women must be able to lie down and rest under suitable conditions during breaks and, where necessary, during working time (ArbStättV Nr. 4.2 Abs. 1; §9 Abs. 3 MuSchG); facilities must be at the workplace or in its immediate vicinity, in sufficient number to ensure availability at all times, with privacy, padded and with a washable or disposable cover (ASR A4.2 point 6 (2)) — [KomNet 42495](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/42495), [betriebsarzt.online](https://www.betriebsarzt.online/de/blog/ruheraum-fur-schwangere-mitarbeiterinnen-und-stillende-muetter/)
- Reachability: break rooms must not be more than 5 minutes' walk away; break areas max 100 m — [KomNet 28752](https://www.komnet.nrw.de/_sitetools/dialog/28752)
- ASR A4.2 was amended (BAuA change document) — [BAuA ASR A4.2 Änderungen (PDF)](https://www.baua.de/DE/Angebote/Regelwerk/ASR/pdf/ASR-A4-2-Aenderungen-2.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=1); official text — [BAuA ASR A4.2 (PDF)](https://www.baua.de/DE/Angebote/Regelwerk/ASR/pdf/ASR-A4-2.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3)
- Shift work in hospitals: BAuA reports that 51% of hospital nursing staff have rest periods shorter than 11 hours at least once a month and considers permanent night work in nursing "besonders problematisch" — [BAuA A8 (PDF)](https://www.baua.de/DE/Angebote/Publikationen/Praxis/A8.pdf?__blob=publicationFile); §5 Abs. 2 ArbZG allows the 11-hour rest to be cut by one hour in hospitals/care homes with compensation — [pflegia.de](https://www.pflegia.de/magazin/article/nachtdienst-in-der-pflege-gesetze/)

### Inferences
- For §3 Nr. 34 EStG, a cabin stay by itself is a facility, not a certified behavioural-prevention course; the credible path is to wrap cabin time into a ZPP-certified course in the "Stress- und Ressourcenmanagement" field (e.g. an 8-10 unit sleep-hygiene/relaxation programme delivered by a certified course leader, with cabin sessions as practice units) or to position HAVN as a venue used by an employer's own §20b-type BGF measure. Whether the tax authority accepts the cabin component is untested; a binding ruling (Anrufungsauskunft, §42e EStG) would be needed.
- The Sachbezug route is the lowest-friction employer budget: HAVN only needs to become an acceptance partner of a Sachbezugskarte issuer (Edenred, Spendit, Belonio, etc.) or issue its own restricted-use vouchers; EUR 50/month covers one 2-hour stay plus a top-up or a membership at EUR 39.
- The ASR A4.2 "immediate vicinity" requirement means a HAVN station in the same building (or within a few minutes' walk) could plausibly serve as an employer's lying-down facility for pregnant/nursing staff and as a Bereitschaftsraum for on-call staff, but only if availability "at all times" is contractually guaranteed (reserved cabins), which is a strong argument for reserved-capacity contracts with building tenants.

### Gaps
- The BMF application letter on §3 Nr. 34 EStG (20 April 2021, listing non-eligible items such as gym membership fees and massages) could not be retrieved; whether "rest/recovery facilities" are explicitly excluded is unverified.
- No source confirms that any Sachbezugskarte issuer currently lists a rest/nap venue as an acceptance partner.
- No ruling or guidance was found on whether an external, third-party rest facility can discharge the employer's §9 MuSchG / ASR A4.2 duty.

## KQ3. Which travel, loyalty and lounge networks have added non-airport partner venues, and under what terms?

### Takeaway
Lounge networks already list sleep products (Minute Suites, GoSleep, Sleep 'n fly) and Priority Pass has added railway-station lounges (Russia, 2018/2019), while Collinson's SmartDelay sells delay-triggered vouchers for "lounge or travel experience such as spa, sleep pods, or dining" to insurers (Allianz Partners, Hiscox, Sompo, Cathay Life). Terms are typically "one complimentary hour, then discounted rate" and are negotiated per venue; no European city-centre rest cabin is currently listed, and no Deutsche Bahn or Lufthansa rest-product bundling was found.

## KQ4. What do hosts (landlords, coworking, stations, shopping centres) pay for or expect from an amenity operator, and are there revenue-share precedents?

### Takeaway
In German commercial real estate the revenue-share/management-agreement model is established for flex-office operators (Industrious/Brookfield Frankfurt Atrium Tower; "waterfall" splits where the landlord takes most income until base rent, then 50/50), and DB InfraGO and ECE are large commercial landlords with pop-up and flexible-term formats. No precedent of a landlord paying a rest-cabin operator was found; the documented host economics are rent or revenue share, with the landlord bearing fit-out risk under management agreements.

### Cited Findings
- Under coworking management agreements the landlord takes a share of the space's income instead of fixed rent; the operator is paid a management fee as a flat fee, a revenue share, or both — [Coworks](https://www.coworks.com/blog/management-agreements-and-coworking-operations), [Wealth Management](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/wealth-management-industry-trends/office-landlords-consider-management-agreements-in-lieu-of-leases-for-co-working-operators)
- Typical "waterfall": operating costs first, then the landlord receives 80% of income until base rent is reached, then 50/50; splits step up once market rent/occupancy targets are hit — [Proximity](https://www.proximity.space/2021/02/09/coworking-space-management-agreements/), [DeskWorks](https://mydeskworks.com/coworking-news/intro-to-management-agreements-for-coworking-space-operators/)
- Industrious has management agreements with about 40 large office owners; in Germany it took former WeWork space in Frankfurt's Atrium Tower (Brookfield) under a "lease agreement structure with revenue sharing"; a Frankfurt commentator warns the landlord pays out of pocket if occupancy fails to cover the management fee and the usually seven-figure fit-out — [REFIRE](https://www.refire-online.com/investment/german-flex-offices-abandon-traditional-leases-for-revenue-sharing/); a panel estimate claims NOI 30-40% higher than conventionally leased space — [Commercial Property Executive](https://www.commercialsearch.com/news/how-landlords-are-reaping-the-benefits-of-coworking/); CBRE describes providers' shift from leases to partnerships — [CBRE](https://www.cbre.com/insights/books/awakening-an-era-of-flexibility-flexible-office-space-2022/03-provider-transformation)
- DB InfraGO's Geschäftsbereich Personenbahnhöfe leases commercial space in and around stations: about 600 station buildings and more than 830,000 m² of leased space, marketed through regional Vermietungsmanagement — [DB InfraGO](https://www.dbinfrago.com/web/bahnhoefe/leistungen/vermietung-reisendenversorgung-werbung-11340378), [Deutsche Bahn](https://www.deutschebahn.com/de/geschaefte/infrastruktur/bahnhof); station revenue sources include gastronomy, retail, toilets, vending machines and lounges — [eisenbahn.guru](https://eisenbahn.guru/system/infrastruktur/bahnhoefe/die-vermarktung-der-bahnhoefe/); listings run on [bahnliegenschaften.de](https://www.bahnliegenschaften.de/) (example: ~200 m² office in Hamburg-Altona station at EUR 1,500 cold rent)
- ECE leased about 770,000 m² in shopping centres in 2025 — [Konii](https://www.konii.de/news/ece-hat-2025-rund-770-000-m%C2%B2-flaeche-in-shopping-centern-vermietet/); ECE's mall-retail programme offers pop-up and food-kiosk spaces and "neuartige und flexible Mietzeitkonzepte" — [ECE Mallmarketing](https://mallmarketing.ece.com/mallretail-flaechen/); ECE's mobile "Flex Retail Labs" can be deployed short-term in any centre for up to two years (2024) — [stores+shops](https://www.stores-shops.de/konzept/store-konzepte/ladenflaechen-mieten-statt-kaufen/); third-party broker Popretail offers mall space from a few days to several months — [Popretail](https://www.popretail.de/leistungen)
- Coworking amenities in Berlin: Mindspace Hausvogteiplatz lists weekly yoga, a soda/coffee bar and a dedicated maternity room (no nap room) — [Mindspace](https://www.mindspace.me/de/berlin/hausvogteiplatz/office-spaces/); WeWork Friedrichstraße has a parent-child room — [Qonto](https://qonto.com/de/blog/selbststaendige/tipps/coworking-spaces-berlin); a coworking guide lists "nap nooks" among WeWork/Mindspace amenities without naming a Berlin site — [OneCoworking](https://onecoworking.com/blog/wework-vs-mindspace-2025-ultimate-guide-to-coworking-spaces-in-germany)
- Amenity-as-a-service precedent: Framery Subscribed pods are rented to businesses for a fixed monthly fee with maintenance included; no landlord-shared model is documented — [Framery](https://framery.com/en/framery-subscribed/); Framery smart pods report anonymised occupancy data with no PII — [Framery One](https://framery.com/en-us/office-pods-and-booths/framery-one/)
- Dayuse (host-side precedent for monetising idle rooms): charges commission only after the hotel is paid, setup is free, hotels set a gross rate that includes the commission, minimum 30% discount vs. overnight rate; USD 250M business volume in 2025 across 10,500 partner hotels — [Dayuse partners](https://www.dayuse.com/partners), [Dayuse/Mews guide](https://partners-blog.dayuse.com/the-hotel-guide-for-integrating-dayuse-and-mews), [Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/opinion/4132156/your-hotel-is-empty-for-six-hours-a-day-heres-what-that-costing-you)

### Inferences
- For office landlords, HAVN can be pitched as a tenant-retention amenity using the flex-office template: landlord funds fit-out (seven-figure risk is theirs in flex deals, so a cabin station at a fraction of that is attractive), HAVN operates for a management fee plus a waterfall revenue share. For stations and malls, the realistic structure is conventional or turnover-based rent to DB InfraGO/ECE, since those landlords monetise space rather than buy amenities.
- Tenant-level "reserved cabin" contracts (a building's tenants buy guaranteed capacity for pregnant staff, on-call staff and visitors) would combine the Framery subscription logic with the ASR A4.2 duty and give hosts a reason to co-market HAVN.

### Gaps
- No documented case of a landlord, coworking operator or station operator paying a rest/nap operator (as opposed to charging rent) was found; precedents are all flex-office or retail.
- Rent levels per m² for DB station or ECE mall space and ECE turnover-rent terms are not public; Dayuse's commission percentage is not disclosed.
- No Berlin coworking site was confirmed to operate a nap room.

## KQ6. Health-system and data/research partners: insurers' prevention budgets and bonus programmes, occupational health, DiGA referral, and data partnerships (Nine Hours/NTT Data, Withings), including consent terms

### Takeaway
German statutory insurers pay for certified prevention courses (TK: 80%, max EUR 150, two per year) and run bonus programmes that reward gym/club membership and courses (TK: EUR 10-20 per measure), while private insurers (Generali Vitality) reward partner purchases; sleep DiGAs (somnio EUR 224.99 per 90-day prescription) show that insurers already fund sleep interventions. The Nine Hours/NTT Data capsule-hotel model shows a sleep-data partnership at scale (over 100,000 consenting guests, 10-million-person target by 2027) built on opt-in consent and anonymisation; Withings' policy illustrates the standard GDPR structure (separate, withdrawable consent for research; HDS-certified health-data hosting).

### Inferences
- The insurer channel is indirect: HAVN cannot bill GKV insurers directly, but a ZPP-certified sleep/relaxation course run at HAVN would be reimbursable (up to EUR 150 per participant at TK) and bonus-eligible, and the course can be sold to employers under §3 Nr. 34 EStG at the same time, i.e. one certification unlocks both budgets.
- A Nine Hours-style data partnership (NTT Data) is the strongest revenue-plus-credibility precedent for the Health Pod; the standard terms are opt-in consent per stay, anonymisation before third-party sharing, a personal sleep report as the user's benefit, and a research partner (data company or university) funding the sensors. In the EU this is Art. 9 GDPR special-category data, so explicit consent, a DPIA and EU/HDS-grade hosting (as Withings does) would be the baseline.
- DiGA makers (somnio/ResMed, HelloBetter, somnovia) and sleep clinics are referral rather than revenue partners; the sensible model is co-marketing (cabin trial for DiGA users, sleep-clinic patients practising sleep hygiene) rather than payment flows.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of a German insurer (statutory or private) paying for or rewarding rest-facility use directly; Generali Vitality's current 2025/26 partner list was not retrievable.
- No German hospital, airport, police or rail pilot of nap rooms or sleep cabins for shift staff was found; the only shift-work rest precedents are academic studies abroad and Zappos' nap room.
- No published revenue figures for the Nine Hours/NTT Data data partnership and no public template for sleep-data consent terms in the EU were found; the Withings policy is the closest documented practice.
- University sleep-lab partnership terms and SleepScore Labs B2B terms were not found.
